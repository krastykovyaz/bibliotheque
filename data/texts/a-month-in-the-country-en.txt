## I · The Drawing-Room

The scene is a drawing-room. On the right, a card table and the door to the study; straight ahead, the door to the ballroom; on the left, two windows and a round table. Sofas stand in the corners. At the card table Anna Semyonovna, Lizaveta Bogdanovna and Schaaf are playing preference; at the round table sit Natalya Petrovna and Rakitin. Natalya Petrovna is embroidering on canvas; Rakitin holds a book. The wall clock shows three.

SCHAAF. In hearts.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Again? Really, my good man, you will quite beat us at this rate.

SCHAAF. (Phlegmatically.) Eight in hearts.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (To Lizaveta Bogdanovna.) What a man! One cannot play with him.

(Lizaveta Bogdanovna smiles.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Why did you stop? Read.

RAKITIN. (Raising the book slowly.) “Monte Cristo sprang up, panting...” Natalya Petrovna, does this interest you?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Not in the least.

RAKITIN. Then why are we reading it?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I will tell you why. The other day a lady said to me: “You have not read Monte Cristo? Oh, do read it, it is delightful.” I answered nothing at the time, but now I can tell her that I have read it and found no delight in it at all.

RAKITIN. Well, yes, if you have already managed to convince yourself...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh, how lazy you are!

RAKITIN. I am ready, I assure you... (Finding the place where he stopped.) “Sprang up, panting, and...”

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him.) Have you seen Arkady today?

RAKITIN. I met him at the dam. They are mending it for you. He was explaining something to the workmen, and for the sake of greater clearness he waded into the sand up to his knees.

NATALYA PETROVNA. He throws himself into everything with too much heat... he tries too hard. It is a fault. Don't you think so?

RAKITIN. I agree with you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. How tiresome! You always agree with me. Read.

RAKITIN. Ah! So you want me to argue with you... Very well.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I want... I want! I want you to want... Read, I tell you.

RAKITIN. I obey. (He takes up the book again.)

SCHAAF. In hearts.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. What? Again? This is intolerable! (To Natalya Petrovna.) Natasha... Natasha...

NATALYA PETROVNA. What is it?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Just imagine, Schaaf has quite beaten us... Seven, eight in hearts every time.

SCHAAF. And the double seven.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Do you hear? It is dreadful.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes... dreadful.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Then let us play whist. (To Natalya Petrovna.) And where is Kolya?

NATALYA PETROVNA. He has gone for a walk with the new tutor.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Ah! Lizaveta Bogdanovna, I invite you.

RAKITIN. (To Natalya Petrovna.) What tutor?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh yes! I forgot to tell you... we hired a new tutor while you were away.

RAKITIN. In place of Dufour?

NATALYA PETROVNA. No... a Russian tutor. The Princess will send us a Frenchman from Moscow.

RAKITIN. What sort of man is he, this Russian? Is he old?

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, young... We have taken him only for the summer months, though.

RAKITIN. Ah! On a trial basis.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, that is what they call it, I believe. And do you know what, Rakitin? You like to observe people, to analyze them, to dig about in them...

RAKITIN. Good heavens, what makes you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, yes... Turn your attention to him. I like him. Thin, slender, a cheerful glance, a bold expression... You will see. He is, it is true, rather awkward... and for you that is a calamity.

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, you persecute me terribly today.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Joking aside, do notice him. It seems to me he may turn out a fine man. But then, who knows!

RAKITIN. You arouse my curiosity...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Really? (She grows thoughtful.) Read.

RAKITIN. “Sprang up, panting, and...”

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Suddenly looking round.) And where is Vera? I have not seen her since morning. (Smiling to Rakitin.) Put that book away... I see we shall not manage any reading today... Tell me something instead...

RAKITIN. Very well... What shall I tell you? You know I spent a few days at the Krinitsyns'. Imagine, our young couple are already bored.

NATALYA PETROVNA. How could you tell?

RAKITIN. Can boredom be hidden? Everything else can be... but not boredom.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Looking at him.) Everything else can be hidden?

RAKITIN. (After a short silence.) I think so.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Lowering her eyes.) Then what did you do at the Krinitsyns'?

RAKITIN. Nothing. To be bored with one's friends is a terrible thing: you are comfortable, unconstrained, you love them, you have nothing to be angry about, and yet boredom wears you down, and the heart aches stupidly, like a hungry man.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You must often be bored with friends.

RAKITIN. As if you did not know yourself what it is to have beside you a person one loves, and who is tiresome!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Slowly.) A person one loves... that is a great word. You say something very subtle.

RAKITIN. Subtle... why subtle?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, that is your fault. Do you know, Rakitin, you are certainly very clever, but... (She stops.) sometimes you and I talk as if we were making lace. Have you ever seen how lace is made? In stuffy rooms, without moving from the spot... Lace is a lovely thing, but a draught of fresh water on a hot day is far better.

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, you are today...

NATALYA PETROVNA. What?

RAKITIN. You are angry with me today about something.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh, subtle people, how little you perceive, subtle as you are!... No, I am not angry with you.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Ah! At last he is caught! He has fallen in! (To Natalya Petrovna.) Natasha, our villain has gone down.

SCHAAF. (Sourly.) It is Lizaveta Bogdanovna's fault...

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Heatedly.) Excuse me, I could not know that Anna Semyonovna had no hearts.

SCHAAF. In future I do not invite Lizaveta Bogdanovna.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (To Schaaf.) But how is she to blame?

SCHAAF. (Repeating in exactly the same voice.) In future I do not invite Lizaveta Bogdanovna.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. What do I care! Well, really!...

RAKITIN. The more I look at you, Natalya Petrovna, the less I recognize your face today.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some curiosity.) Really?

RAKITIN. Truly. I find some change in you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes?... In that case do me a favour... You know me, guess what this change consists in, what has happened to me, eh?

RAKITIN. Wait a moment...

(Kolya suddenly runs in noisily from the ballroom, straight to Anna Semyonovna.)

KOLYA. Grandmother, grandmother! Look what I have! (Showing her a bow and arrows.) Look!

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Show me, my darling... Oh, what a fine bow! Who made it for you?

KOLYA. There he is... he did. (Pointing to Belyaev, who has stopped at the door of the ballroom.)

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Ah! How well it is made...

KOLYA. I have already shot at a tree with it, grandmother, and hit it twice... (He jumps about.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Show me, Kolya.

KOLYA. (Running to her, while Natalya Petrovna examines the bow.) Oh, maman! How Alexei Nikolayich climbs trees! He wants to teach me, and he will teach me to swim too. He will teach me everything, everything! (He jumps about.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Belyaev.) I am very grateful to you for your attention to Kolya...

KOLYA. (Eagerly interrupting her.) I love him very much, maman, very much!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Stroking Kolya's head.) He is a little too delicate. Make him into an agile, nimble boy for me.

(Belyaev bows.)

KOLYA. Alexei Nikolayich, let us go to the stable and take some bread to Favorite.

BELYAEV. Let us go.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (To Kolya.) Come here, kiss me first...

KOLYA. (Running away.) Afterwards, grandmother, afterwards!

(He runs off into the ballroom; Belyaev goes out after him.)

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Looking after Kolya.) What a darling child! (To Schaaf and Lizaveta Bogdanovna.) Isn't he?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Indeed he is.

SCHAAF. (After a short pause.) I pass.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Rakitin, with some animation.) Well, how did he strike you?

RAKITIN. Who?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) This... Russian tutor.

RAKITIN. Ah, forgive me, I had forgotten... I was so busy with the question you put to me...

(Natalya Petrovna looks at him with a barely noticeable smile.)

Though his face... indeed... Yes, he has a good face. I like him. Only he seems to be very shy.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes.

RAKITIN. (Looking at her.) But all the same I cannot account for it to myself...

NATALYA PETROVNA. What if we took him up, Rakitin? Would you like to? Let us complete his education. Here is an excellent opportunity for sedate, reasonable people like you and me! For we are very reasonable, are we not?

RAKITIN. This young man interests you. If he knew it... he would be flattered.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh, believe me, not in the least! One cannot judge him by what... our sort would do in his place. He is not at all like us, Rakitin. That is just the trouble, my friend: we study ourselves with great diligence and then imagine that we know people.

RAKITIN. Another's soul is a dark forest. But why these hints... Why do you keep pricking me?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Whom should one prick if not one's friends... And you are my friend... You know it. (She presses his hand.)

(Rakitin smiles and brightens.)

You are my old friend.

RAKITIN. I am only afraid... that this old friend may pall on you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Laughing.) Only good things pall.

RAKITIN. Perhaps... But that does not make it easier for them.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Come now... (Lowering her voice.) As though you did not know... what you are to me.

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, you play with me like a cat with a mouse... But the mouse does not complain.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh, poor little mouse! (To Anna Semyonovna.) Twenty from you, Adam Ivanych... Aha!

SCHAAF. I do not invite Lizaveta Bogdanovna in future.

MATVEI. (Entering from the ballroom and announcing.) Ignaty Ilyich has arrived.

SHPIGELSKY. (Coming in at his heels.) One does not announce doctors.

(Matvei goes out.)

My humblest respects to the whole family. (Going up to kiss Anna Semyonovna's hand.) Good day, madam. In the money, I trust?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. In the money! I barely won back my losses... and thank God for that! It is all this villain's doing. (Pointing to Schaaf.)

SHPIGELSKY. (To Schaaf.) Adam Ivanych, with the ladies! That is not nice... I do not recognize you.

SCHAAF. (Grumbling through his teeth.) With ladies, with ladies...

SHPIGELSKY. (Going up to the round table on the left.) Good day, Natalya Petrovna! Good day, Mikhailo Alexandrych!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Good day, doctor. How are you getting on?

SHPIGELSKY. I like that question very much... It means you are well. What can happen to me? A decent doctor is never ill; he may all of a sudden take it into his head to die... Ha, ha.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Sit down. I am well, truly... but I am out of spirits... and that too is a kind of illness.

SHPIGELSKY. (Sitting beside Natalya Petrovna.) Allow me your pulse... (He feels her pulse.) Oh, these nerves of mine, nerves... You walk too little, Natalya Petrovna... you laugh too little... that is it... Mikhailo Alexandrych, what are you staring at? However, we might prescribe the white drops.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I have nothing against laughing... (Briskly.) Now you, doctor... you have a wicked tongue, I like you for it very much and respect you, truly... tell me something funny. Mikhailo Alexandrych is philosophizing all day today.

SHPIGELSKY. (Stealing a glance at Rakitin.) Ah, so it is not only the nerves that suffer; the bile too is a little stirred up...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, you too! Observe as much as you like, doctor, but not aloud. We all know that you are terribly penetrating... You are both very penetrating.

SHPIGELSKY. I obey.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Tell us something funny.

SHPIGELSKY. I obey. Here I never thought, never dreamed, and bang, tell a story... Allow me a pinch of snuff. (He takes snuff.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. What preparations!

SHPIGELSKY. But, my dear Natalya Petrovna, be so good as to consider: funny is not the same as funny. It depends on the person. Your neighbour, for instance, Mr. Khlopushkin, has only to have a finger held up like this, and off he goes, wheezing and weeping with laughter... but you... Well, however, allow me. Do you know Verenitsyn, Platon Vasilyevich?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I think I do, or I have heard of him.

SHPIGELSKY. He has a sister who is mad, too. In my opinion they are either both mad or both of sound mind, because between brother and sister there is decidedly no difference, but that is not the point. Fate, madam, fate everywhere, fate in everything. Verenitsyn has a daughter, a greenish sort of girl, you know, pale little eyes, red little nose, yellow little teeth, in a word a most agreeable young lady; she plays the pianoforte and lisps as well, so everything is in order. She has two hundred souls, and her aunt's hundred and fifty besides. The aunt is still alive and will live long, mad people all live long, but every trouble can be mended. She signed a will in favour of her niece, and on the eve I had poured cold water on her head with my own hands, quite needlessly, as it happens, because there is no possibility of curing her. Well then, Verenitsyn has a daughter, a bride not of the worst. He began taking her out, suitors began to appear, and among them a certain Perekuzov, an anaemic young man, timid, but of excellent principles. So then, our Perekuzov pleased the father; he pleased the daughter... What, it seems, could be the hindrance? God bless you, to the altar! And indeed everything went splendidly: Mr. Verenitsyn, Platon Vasilyevich, was already beginning to slap Mr. Perekuzov on the stomach, you know, and pat him on the shoulder, when suddenly from out of nowhere a visiting officer turns up, Ardalion Protobekasov! At the Marshal's ball he saw Verenitsyn's daughter, danced three polkas with her, and said to her, no doubt rolling his eyes like this: “Oh, how unhappy I am!” and my young lady went off her head at once. Tears began, sighs, groans... They don't look at Perekuzov, they don't speak to Perekuzov, at the mere word “wedding” she goes into convulsions... Good Lord, what a puzzle! Well, thinks Verenitsyn, if it is Protobekasov, then Protobekasov it is. He too is a man of means, after all. They invite Protobekasov, saying, pray do us the honour... Protobekasov does the honour; Protobekasov arrives, dangles about, falls in love, and finally offers his hand and heart. And what do you think? Does Miss Verenitsyn accept at once with joy? Not a bit of it! God forbid! Again tears, sighs, fits. The father is at his wits' end. What, finally? What does she want? And what do you think she answers him? I don't know, papa, she says, whom I love, this one or that one. “How?” Truly, I don't know, and I had rather marry neither, but I love! Verenitsyn naturally has an attack of cholera on the spot, the suitors too don't know what in the world is going on, and she stands by her own. There, madam, be so good as to judge what marvels take place among us!

NATALYA PETROVNA. I find nothing surprising in that... As if one could not love two people at once?

RAKITIN. Ah! You think...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Slowly.) I think... but then, I do not know... perhaps it proves only that one loves neither the one nor the other.

SHPIGELSKY. (Taking snuff and glancing now at Natalya Petrovna, now at Rakitin.) So that is how it is, so that is how it is...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Briskly, to Shpigelsky.) Your story is very good, but all the same you have not made me laugh.

SHPIGELSKY. Well, my dear lady, who could make you laugh now, I ask you? It is not that you need now.

NATALYA PETROVNA. What do I need, then?

SHPIGELSKY. (With pretended humility.) God only knows!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh, how tedious you are, no better than Rakitin.

SHPIGELSKY. Too much honour, I assure you...

(Natalya Petrovna makes an impatient movement.)

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Rising from her seat.) Well, at last. (She sighs.) I have sat my legs quite numb.

(Lizaveta Bogdanovna and Schaaf rise too.)

Oh dear.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Rising and going over to them.) Fancy wanting to sit so long...

(Shpigelsky and Rakitin rise.)

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (To Schaaf.) You owe me seven hryvnas, my good man.

(Schaaf bows stiffly.)

It is not always for you to punish us. (To Natalya Petrovna.) You look rather pale today, Natasha. Are you well?... Shpigelsky, is she well?

SHPIGELSKY. (Who has been whispering something with Rakitin.) Oh, perfectly!

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. That's all right, then... I shall go and rest a little before dinner... I am tired to death. Liza, come... oh, my legs, my legs...

(She goes into the ballroom with Lizaveta Bogdanovna. Natalya Petrovna sees her to the door. Shpigelsky, Rakitin and Schaaf remain at the front of the stage.)

SHPIGELSKY. (To Schaaf, offering him his snuffbox.) Well, Adam Ivanych, how do you find yourself?

SCHAAF. (Taking snuff with gravity.) Very well. And you?

SHPIGELSKY. Humbly thank you, so-so. (To Rakitin, in a low voice.) So you really do not know what is the matter with Natalya Petrovna today?

RAKITIN. Truly, I do not know.

SHPIGELSKY. Well, if you do not know... (He turns and goes to meet Natalya Petrovna, who is coming back from the door.) I have a little matter to discuss with you, Natalya Petrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Going to the window.) Really? What is it?

SHPIGELSKY. I need to speak with you privately...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Indeed... you frighten me.

(Meanwhile Rakitin has taken Schaaf by the arm and walks up and down with him, whispering something to him in German. Schaaf laughs and says quietly: “Yes, yes, yes, certainly, certainly, very good.”)

SHPIGELSKY. (Lowering his voice.) The matter, strictly speaking, does not concern you alone...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Looking out into the garden.) What do you mean?

SHPIGELSKY. It is like this, madam. A good acquaintance of mine asked me to find out... that is... your intentions regarding your ward... Vera Alexandrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. My intentions?

SHPIGELSKY. That is... to speak without circumlocution, my acquaintance...

NATALYA PETROVNA. He is not asking for her hand?

SHPIGELSKY. Exactly so.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You are joking?

SHPIGELSKY. Not at all.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Laughing.) But for heaven's sake, she is still a child; what a strange commission!

SHPIGELSKY. What is strange about it, Natalya Petrovna? My acquaintance...

NATALYA PETROVNA. You are a great man of business, Shpigelsky. And who is your acquaintance?

SHPIGELSKY. (Smiling.) Allow me, allow me. You have not yet told me anything positive regarding...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Enough, doctor. Vera is still a child. You know it yourself, Mr. Diplomat. (Turning round.) Why, here she is, by the way.

(Vera and Kolya run in from the ballroom.)

KOLYA. (Running to Rakitin.) Rakitin, order some glue for us, glue...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Vera.) Where have you come from? (She strokes her cheek.) How flushed you are...

VERA. From the garden...

(Shpigelsky bows to her.)

Good day, Ignaty Ilyich.

RAKITIN. (To Kolya.) What do you want glue for?

KOLYA. We need it, we need it... Alexei Nikolayich is making us a kite... Please order it...

RAKITIN. (About to ring the bell.) Wait, in a moment...

SCHAAF. Allow me... Master Kolya has not recited his lesson today. (He takes Kolya by the hand.) Come, sir.

KOLYA. (Mournfully.) Tomorrow, Herr Schaaf, tomorrow...

SCHAAF. (Sharply.) Tomorrow, tomorrow, not today, all the lazy people say... Come, sir...

(Kolya resists.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Vera.) Who were you walking with for so long? I have not seen you since morning.

VERA. With Alexei Nikolayich... with Kolya...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! (Turning round.) Kolya, what is the meaning of this?

KOLYA. (Lowering his voice.) Herr Schaaf... Mamma...

RAKITIN. (To Natalya Petrovna.) They are making a kite out there, and here they want to give him a lesson.

SCHAAF. (With dignity.) Gracious lady...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Sternly, to Kolya.) You will do as you are told, you have run about enough today... Go with Herr Schaaf.

SCHAAF. (Leading Kolya into the ballroom.) It is unheard of!

KOLYA. (Going out, in a whisper to Rakitin.) But all the same, order the glue...

(Rakitin nods.)

SCHAAF. (Tugging Kolya.) Come, sir...

(He goes into the ballroom with him. Rakitin goes out after them.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Vera.) Sit down... you must be tired... (She sits down herself.)

VERA. (Sitting down.) Not at all, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Smiling, to Shpigelsky.) Shpigelsky, look at her, isn't she tired?

SHPIGELSKY. But it is good for Vera Alexandrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I am not saying it is not... for Vera. Well, what did you do in the garden?

VERA. We played, ma'am; we ran about. First we watched them digging the dam, and then Alexei Nikolayich climbed a tree after a squirrel, high, high up, and began to shake the top... We were all quite frightened... The squirrel fell at last, and Trezor very nearly caught it... but it got away.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Smiling and glancing at Shpigelsky.) And then?

VERA. And then Alexei Nikolayich made Kolya a bow... so quickly... and then he crept up to our cow in the meadow and suddenly jumped on her back... the cow took fright and ran off, kicking... and he laughs (she laughs herself), and then Alexei Nikolayich wanted to make us a kite, so we came here.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Patting her cheek.) Child, child, a perfect child... eh? What do you think, Shpigelsky?

SHPIGELSKY. (Slowly, looking at Natalya Petrovna.) I agree with you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. There, you see.

SHPIGELSKY. But it does not prevent anything... On the contrary...

NATALYA PETROVNA. You think so? (To Vera.) Well, did you enjoy yourself very much?

VERA. Yes, ma'am... Alexei Nikolayich is so amusing.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I see. (After a short pause.) Verochka, how old are you?

(Vera looks at her with some astonishment.)

Child... child...

(Rakitin comes in from the ballroom.)

SHPIGELSKY. (Fussily.) Oh, I had forgotten... your coachman is ill... and I have not yet seen him...

NATALYA PETROVNA. What is the matter with him?

SHPIGELSKY. A fever; but there is no danger at all.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After him.) Will you dine with us, doctor?

SHPIGELSKY. If you permit. (He goes out into the ballroom.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. My child, you would do well to put on another dress for dinner...

(Vera rises.)

Come to me... (She kisses her on the forehead.) Child, child! (Vera kisses her hand and goes into the study.)

RAKITIN. (Softly, to Vera, with a wink.) And I have sent Alexei Nikolayich everything he needs.

VERA. (In a low voice.) Thank you, Mikhail Alexandrych. (She goes out.)

RAKITIN. (Going up to Natalya Petrovna. She holds out her hand to him. He presses it at once.) At last we are alone... Natalya Petrovna, tell me, what is the matter with you?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Nothing, Michel, nothing. And if there was anything, it is all over now. Sit down.

(Rakitin sits down beside her.)

Who has not had such moods? Clouds do pass across the sky. Why are you looking at me so?

RAKITIN. I am looking at you... I am happy.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Smiling back at him.) Open the window, Michel. How lovely it is in the garden!

(Rakitin rises and opens the window.)

Hello, wind. (She laughs.) He seems to have been waiting for a chance to burst in... (Looking round.) How he has taken possession of the whole room... Now you will not drive him out...

RAKITIN. You yourself are now as gentle and quiet as the evening after a storm.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Thoughtfully repeating the last words.) After a storm... But was there a storm?

RAKITIN. (Shaking his head.) One was gathering.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Really? (Looking at him, after a short silence.) Do you know, Michel, I cannot imagine a kinder man than you. Truly.

(Rakitin tries to stop her.)

No, do not hinder me from speaking out. You are indulgent, affectionate, constant. You do not change. I owe you much.

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, why do you say this to me just now?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I do not know; I am cheerful, I am resting; do not forbid me to chatter...

RAKITIN. (Pressing her hand.) You are kind as an angel.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Laughing.) This morning you would not have said so... But listen, Michel, you know me, you must excuse me. Our relations are so pure, so sincere... and yet not quite natural. We have the right to look straight in the eyes not only of Arkady but of everybody... Yes; but... (She grows thoughtful.) That is why I sometimes feel so heavy and so awkward, I get angry, I am ready, like a child, to vent my vexation on someone else, especially on you... Does this preference not anger you?

RAKITIN. (Eagerly.) On the contrary...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, sometimes it is pleasant to torment the one you love... the one you love... For I, like Tatyana, can also say: “Why dissemble?”

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him.) Yes... I love you; but do you know what, Rakitin? Do you know what seems strange to me at times: I love you... and this feeling is so clear, so peaceful. It does not stir me... I am warmed by it, but... (Briskly.) You have never made me cry... and it seems to me I should have... (Breaking off.) What does that mean?

RAKITIN. (Rather sadly.) Such a question needs no answer.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Thoughtfully.) And yet we have known each other so long.

RAKITIN. Four years. Yes, we are old friends.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Friends... No, you are more to me than a friend...

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, do not touch upon this question... I am afraid for my happiness, lest it vanish in your hands.

NATALYA PETROVNA. No... no... no. The whole point is that you are too kind... You indulge me too much... You have spoiled me... You are too kind, do you hear?

RAKITIN. (With a smile.) I obey.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Looking at him.) I do not know how you... I wish for no other happiness... Many might envy me. (She holds out both hands to him.) Isn't it so?

RAKITIN. I am in your power... make of me what you will...

(Islayev's voice is heard in the ballroom.)

ISLAYEV. (Offstage.) So you have sent for him?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Rising quickly.) He! I cannot see him now... Farewell! (She goes into the study.)

RAKITIN. (Looking after her.) What is this? The beginning of the end or simply the end? (After a short pause.) Or the beginning?

(Islayev comes in with a preoccupied air and takes off his hat.)

ISLAYEV. Hello, Michel.

RAKITIN. We have already met today.

ISLAYEV. Ah! Excuse me... I am quite harassed. (He walks about the room.) A strange thing! The Russian peasant is very shrewd, very quick; I respect the Russian peasant... and yet sometimes you tell him, and tell him, and explain, and explain... It seems clear, and yet no use at all. The Russian peasant has not that... that...

RAKITIN. Are you still worrying over the dam?

ISLAYEV. That... so to speak... that love of work is lacking... precisely love is lacking. He will not let you express your opinion properly. “I hear, sir...” And what does “I hear” mean? He simply understood nothing. Look at the German, now, that is another matter! The Russian has no patience. For all that, I respect him... And where is Natasha? Do you know?

RAKITIN. She was here just now.

ISLAYEV. What time is it? It must be time for dinner. On my feet since morning, a pile of business... and I have not even been to the building site today. Time just slips away. Dreadful! I simply cannot get round to everything!

(Rakitin smiles.)

You are laughing at me, I see... Well, what is to be done, brother? Each to his own. I am a practical man, born to be a landowner and nothing more. There was a time when I dreamed of something else; but I came a cropper, brother! Burned my fingers, so! Why does Belyaev not come?

RAKITIN. Who is Belyaev?

ISLAYEV. Our new tutor, the Russian. Still a fair bit of a wild one; well, he will get used to it. A boy not without sense. I asked him today to look at the building work...

(Belyaev enters.)

Ah, and here he is! Well? How is it there? Nobody working, I suppose? Eh?

BELYAEV. Yes, they are working.

ISLAYEV. Have they finished the second frame?

BELYAEV. They have begun the third.

ISLAYEV. And about the courses of logs, did you tell them?

BELYAEV. I told them.

ISLAYEV. Well, and what did they say?

BELYAEV. They say they have never done it any other way.

ISLAYEV. Hm. Is Yermil the carpenter there?

BELYAEV. He is there.

ISLAYEV. Ah!... Well, thank you!

(Natalya Petrovna enters.)

Ah! Natasha! Hello!

RAKITIN. Why are you saying hello to everyone twenty times today?

ISLAYEV. I tell you, I am harassed. Oh, by the way! Haven't I shown you my new winnowing machine? Come along, please; it is interesting. Imagine, a hurricane comes out of it, simply a hurricane. We shall have time before dinner... Will you?

RAKITIN. By all means.

ISLAYEV. And you, Natasha, are you not coming with us?

NATALYA PETROVNA. As if I understood anything about your winnowing machines! Go by yourselves, only mind you do not linger.

ISLAYEV. (Going out with Rakitin.) We shall be back at once...

(Belyaev starts to follow them.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Belyaev.) Where are you going, Alexei Nikolayich?

BELYAEV. I... I...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Though, if you want to take a walk...

BELYAEV. No, I was in the open air the whole morning!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! Then in that case sit down... Sit here. (Pointing to a chair.) We have not yet had a proper talk, Alexei Nikolayich. We have not got acquainted yet.

(Belyaev bows and sits down.)

And I wish to make your acquaintance.

BELYAEV. I... I am very flattered.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With a smile.) You are afraid of me now, I see... but wait, you will get to know me, you will stop being afraid of me. Tell me... tell me, how old are you?

BELYAEV. Twenty-one, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Are your parents living?

BELYAEV. My mother is dead. My father is alive.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And did your mother die long ago?

BELYAEV. Long ago.

NATALYA PETROVNA. But you remember her?

BELYAEV. Of course... I remember.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And does your father live in Moscow?

BELYAEV. No, in the country.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! Have you any brothers... sisters?

BELYAEV. One sister.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Do you love her very much?

BELYAEV. I do. She is much younger than I.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And what is her name?

BELYAEV. Natalya.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Briskly.) Natalya? That is strange. My name is Natalya too... (She stops.) And do you love her very much?

BELYAEV. Yes, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Tell me, how do you find my Kolya?

BELYAEV. He is a very sweet boy.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Isn't he? And so affectionate! He has already managed to become attached to you.

BELYAEV. I am ready to do my best... I am glad...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Now, you see, Alexei Nikolayich, of course I should like to make a capable man of him. I do not know whether I shall succeed, but in any case I want him always to remember the time of his childhood with pleasure. Let him grow up in freedom, that is the main thing. I myself was brought up differently, Alexei Nikolayich; my father was not an evil man, but irritable and strict... everyone in the house, beginning with mamma, was afraid of him. My brother and I used to cross ourselves in secret every time we were summoned to him. Sometimes my father would begin to caress me, but even in his arms I, as I remember, went quite numb. My brother grew up, and you may perhaps have heard of his break with our father... I shall never forget that terrible day... I remained an obedient daughter until my father's death... he called me his comfort, his Antigone... (he went blind in the last years of his life); but his tenderest caresses could not efface the first impressions of my youth... I was afraid of him, a blind old man, and never felt free in his presence... The traces of that timidity, of that long constraint, perhaps have not entirely disappeared even now... I know that at first glance I seem... how shall I put it?... cold, perhaps... But I notice that I am telling you about myself, instead of talking to you about Kolya. I only meant to say that from my own experience I know how good it is for a child to grow up in freedom... You, I think, were not restrained in your childhood, were you?

BELYAEV. How shall I say... No one restrained me, certainly... no one troubled about me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Timidly.) But your father, surely...

BELYAEV. He had no time for that. He was mostly driving round to the neighbours... on business... or even not on business, but... Through them, one might say, he earned his bread. By his services.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! And so no one at all took care of your education?

BELYAEV. To tell the truth, no one. However, it is probably noticeable. I feel my shortcomings only too well.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Perhaps... but then... (She stops and goes on with some embarrassment.) Oh, by the way, Alexei Nikolayich, was it you singing in the garden yesterday?

BELYAEV. When?

NATALYA PETROVNA. In the evening, by the pond. Was it you?

BELYAEV. Yes. (Hastily.) I did not think... the pond is so far from here... I did not think one could be heard here...

NATALYA PETROVNA. But you seem to be apologizing? You have a very pleasant, ringing voice, and you sing so well. Did you study music?

BELYAEV. No, ma'am. I sing by ear... only simple songs.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You sing them beautifully... I shall ask you someday... not now, but when we know each other better, when we have grown closer... for, is it not so, Alexei Nikolayich, we shall grow closer? I feel confidence in you, my chatter may prove it to you.

(She holds out her hand for him to press it. Belyaev takes it hesitantly and, after some bewilderment, not knowing what to do with the hand, kisses it. Natalya Petrovna blushes and withdraws her hand. At this moment Shpigelsky comes in from the ballroom, stops and takes a step back. Natalya Petrovna rises quickly; Belyaev too.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (In confusion.) Ah, it is you, doctor... and here we are with Alexei Nikolayich... (She stops.)

SHPIGELSKY. (Loudly and breezily.) Just imagine, Natalya Petrovna, what goes on in your house. I go into the servants' hall, I ask for the sick coachman, and look! my invalid is sitting at the table stuffing a pancake with onions into both cheeks. After that, practise medicine, rely on illness and honest income!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With a forced smile.) Ah! Really...

(Belyaev wants to go.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Alexei Nikolayich, I forgot to tell you...

VERA. (Running in from the ballroom.) Alexei Nikolayich! Alexei Nikolayich! (She suddenly stops at the sight of Natalya Petrovna.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some surprise.) What is it? What do you want?

VERA. (Blushing and lowering her eyes, pointing to Belyaev.) They are calling him.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Who?

VERA. Kolya... that is, Kolya asked me about the kite...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! (In a low voice to Vera.) One does not burst into a room like that... It is not proper.

(Turning to Shpigelsky.) What time is it, doctor? Your watch is always right... It is time for dinner.

SHPIGELSKY. Allow me. (He takes his watch out of his pocket.) Now, madam... now, I beg to report, it is twenty past four.

NATALYA PETROVNA. There, you see. It is time.

(She goes to the mirror and arranges her hair. Meanwhile Vera whispers something to Belyaev. They both laugh. Natalya Petrovna sees them in the mirror.)

(Shpigelsky watches her sideways.)

BELYAEV. (Laughing, in a low voice.) Really?

VERA. (Nodding, also in a low voice.) Yes, yes, she simply fell.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Turning to Vera with feigned indifference.) What is it? Who fell?

VERA. (Confused.) Nothing... Alexei Nikolayich has made a swing there, and nurse took it into her head...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Not waiting for the end of the answer, to Shpigelsky.) Oh, by the way, Shpigelsky, come here a moment... (She leads him aside and turns to Vera again.) Was she not hurt?

VERA. Oh no!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes... but all the same, Alexei Nikolayich, you should not have...

MATVEI. (Coming in from the ballroom and announcing.) Dinner is served.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! But where is Arkady Sergeich? They will be late again, he and Mikhail Alexandrovich.

MATVEI. They are already in the dining-room.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And mamma?

MATVEI. She is in the dining-room too.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! Then let us go. (Pointing to Belyaev.) Vera, go ahead with the gentleman.

(Matvei goes out, followed by Belyaev and Vera.)

SHPIGELSKY. (To Natalya Petrovna.) Was there something you wanted to tell me?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh yes! Indeed... You see... We shall talk again about... about your proposal.

SHPIGELSKY. Concerning... Vera Alexandrovna?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes. I shall think... I shall think.

(They both go into the ballroom.)

## II · The Garden

The scene is a garden. To the right and left, under the trees, benches; straight ahead, a raspberry patch. Katya and Matvei come in from the right. Katya has a basket in her hand.

MATVEI. Well then, Katerina Vasilyevna? Be so good as to explain yourself at last, I beg you earnestly.

KATYA. Matvei Yegorych, truly, I...

MATVEI. You know only too well, Katerina Vasilyevna, how I am disposed towards you. Of course I am older than you in years; that cannot be disputed; but all the same I can still stand up for myself, I am still in my prime. My temper too, as you are pleased to know, is gentle; what more, it would seem?

KATYA. Matvei Yegorych, believe me, I feel it very much, I am very grateful, Matvei Yegorych... But here... we must wait, I think.

MATVEI. But wait for what, for pity's sake, Katerina Vasilyevna? Formerly, allow me to observe, you did not say that. And as regards respect, I think I can answer for myself. You will receive such respect, Katerina Vasilyevna, as could not be better asked for. Besides, I am a man who does not drink, and I have never heard an ill word from the masters either.

KATYA. Truly, Matvei Yegorych, I do not know what to say to you...

MATVEI. Ah, Katerina Vasilyevna, it is only lately that you have begun to be rather so...

KATYA. (Blushing slightly.) What do you mean, lately? Why lately?

MATVEI. I do not know... but formerly you... you used to treat me differently.

KATYA. (Glancing into the wings, hurriedly.) Take care... the German is coming.

MATVEI. (With vexation.) Bother him, the long-nosed crane!... But I shall talk to you again. (He goes out to the right.)

(Katya also wants to go into the raspberry patch. Schaaf comes in from the left with a fishing rod on his shoulder.)

SCHAAF. (After Katya.) Vere? Vere to, Katerin?

KATYA. (Stopping.) We were told to pick raspberries, Adam Ivanych.

SCHAAF. Ze raspberry... is a pleasant fruit. You like raspberry?

KATYA. Yes, I do.

SCHAAF. Ha, ha!... I too... I too. I like all that you like. (Seeing that she wants to go.) Oh, Katerin, vait a little.

KATYA. I have no time. The housekeeper will scold.

SCHAAF. Eh! Never mind. See, I too am going... (Pointing to his rod.) How do you say, to fish, you understand, to fish, to take fish. You like? Fish?

KATYA. Yes.

SCHAAF. Eh, ha, ha, I too, I too. And do you know what I shall tell you, Katerin... In German there is a little song: “Cathrinchen, Cathrinchen, how dearly I love you!” that is to say: “O Katrinushka, Katrinushka, you are nice, I love you.” (He tries to put one arm round her.)

KATYA. Stop, stop, are you not ashamed... The masters are coming. (She escapes into the raspberry patch.)

SCHAAF. (Assuming a stern air, in a low voice.) That is stupid...

(Natalya Petrovna enters from the right on Rakitin's arm.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Schaaf.) Ah! Adam Ivanych! Are you going fishing?

SCHAAF. Exactly so.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And where is Kolya?

SCHAAF. With Lizaveta Bogdanovna... a lesson on the pianoforte...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! (Looking round.) Are you here alone?

SCHAAF. Alone.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Have you not seen Alexei Nikolayich?

SCHAAF. No.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) Let us go with you, Adam Ivanych, shall we? We shall see how you catch fish.

SCHAAF. I am very glad.

RAKITIN. (In a low voice to Natalya Petrovna.) What a notion!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Rakitin.) Come, come, my gloomy beauty...

(All three go out to the right.)

KATYA. (Cautiously putting her head out of the raspberry patch.) Gone... (She comes out, stops a moment and thinks.) Look at that German!... (She sighs and begins picking raspberries again, humming softly.)

It is not fire that burns, not pitch that boils, it is the eager heart that boils and burns...

But Matvei Yegorych is right! (Humming on.)

It is the eager heart that boils and burns, not for father, not for mother...

What big raspberries... (Humming on.)

Not for father, not for mother... What heat! It is even stifling. (Humming on.)

Not for father, not for mother... It is the eager heart that boils and burns for...

(She suddenly looks round, falls silent and half hides behind a bush. Belyaev and Verochka enter from the left; Belyaev has a kite in his hands.)

BELYAEV. (Passing the raspberry patch, to Katya.) Why did you stop, Katya? (He sings.)

It is the eager heart that boils and burns for a fair maiden...

KATYA. (Blushing.) We do not sing it like that here.

BELYAEV. How, then?

(Katya laughs and does not answer.)

BELYAEV. What, are you picking raspberries? Let me taste.

KATYA. (Giving him the basket.) Take them all...

BELYAEV. Why all?... Vera Alexandrovna, will you have some?

(Vera takes some from the basket, and he takes some.)

BELYAEV. Well, that is enough. (He tries to give the basket back to Katya.)

KATYA. (Pushing his hand away.) But do take them all, take them.

BELYAEV. No, thank you, Katya. (He gives her the basket.) Thank you. (To Vera.) Vera Alexandrovna, let us sit down on the bench. Here, (pointing to the kite) its tail must be tied on. You will help me.

(They both go and sit down on the bench. Belyaev puts the kite into her hands.)

BELYAEV. There. Now mind, hold it straight. (He begins to tie on the tail.) What is it?

VERA. But like that I cannot see you.

BELYAEV. And why should you see me?

VERA. I mean I want to see how you tie the tail.

BELYAEV. Ah! Well, wait. (He arranges the kite so that she can see it.) Katya, why don't you sing? Sing.

(After a short while Katya begins to hum softly.)

VERA. Tell me, Alexei Nikolayich, did you fly kites in Moscow too, sometimes?

BELYAEV. No time for kites in Moscow! Hold the string... so. You think we have nothing else to do in Moscow?

VERA. What do you do in Moscow?

BELYAEV. What do we do? We study, we listen to professors.

VERA. And what do they teach you?

BELYAEV. Everything.

VERA. You must study very well. Better than all the others.

BELYAEV. No, not very well. Better than all the others, indeed! I am lazy.

VERA. Why are you lazy?

BELYAEV. God knows! I was born that way, I suppose.

VERA. (After a pause.) Have you friends in Moscow?

BELYAEV. Of course. Ah, this string is not strong enough.

VERA. And do you love them?

BELYAEV. I should think so!... Do you not love your friends?

VERA. Friends... I have no friends.

BELYAEV. I mean to say, your girl companions.

VERA. (Slowly.) Yes.

BELYAEV. You have girl companions, haven't you?...

VERA. Yes... only I do not know why... for some time now I think very little about them... I did not even answer Liza Moshnina, though she begged me so in her letter.

BELYAEV. How can you say you have no friends... and what am I?

VERA. (With a smile.) Well, you... You are another matter. (After a pause.) Alexei Nikolayich!

BELYAEV. What?

VERA. Do you write verses?

BELYAEV. No. Why?

VERA. Just so. (After a pause.) At our boarding school one young lady wrote verses.

BELYAEV. (Pulling a knot tight with his teeth.) Is that so! And were they good?

VERA. I do not know. She read them to us, and we cried.

BELYAEV. Why did you cry?

VERA. From pity. We were so sorry for her!

BELYAEV. Were you brought up in Moscow?

VERA. In Moscow, at Madame Bolus's. Natalya Petrovna took me from there last year.

BELYAEV. Do you love Natalya Petrovna?

VERA. I do; she is so kind. I love her very much.

BELYAEV. (With a smile.) And you are afraid of her, I dare say?

VERA. (Also smiling.) A little.

BELYAEV. (After a pause.) And who put you in the boarding school?

VERA. Natalya Petrovna's late mother. I grew up in her house. I am an orphan.

BELYAEV. (Letting his hands fall.) You are an orphan? And you remember neither father nor mother?

VERA. No.

BELYAEV. My mother is dead too. We are both orphans, you and I. Well, what of it! We must not lose heart all the same.

VERA. They say orphans soon become friends with one another.

BELYAEV. (Looking her in the eyes.) Really? And what do you think?

VERA. (Also looking him in the eyes, with a smile.) I think they do.

BELYAEV. (Laughing and taking up the kite again.) I should like to know how long I have been in these parts.

VERA. Today is the twenty-eighth day.

BELYAEV. What a memory you have! Well, the kite is finished. Look what a tail! We must go and fetch Kolya.

KATYA. (Coming up to them with her basket.) Will you have more raspberries?

BELYAEV. No, thank you, Katya.

(Katya goes away without a word.)

VERA. Kolya is with Lizaveta Bogdanovna.

BELYAEV. What an idea, to keep a child indoors in such weather!

VERA. Lizaveta Bogdanovna would only be in our way...

BELYAEV. But I am not talking about her...

VERA. (Hastily.) Kolya could not come with us without her... Besides, she spoke of you yesterday with great praise.

BELYAEV. Really?

VERA. Do you not like her?

BELYAEV. Bother her! Let her take her snuff and welcome!... Why are you sighing?

VERA. (After a pause.) Just so. How clear the sky is!

BELYAEV. So that is why you sigh?

(Silence.)

Perhaps you are bored?

VERA. Bored? No! Sometimes I do not myself know what I am sighing for... I am not bored at all. On the contrary... (After a pause.) I do not know... I must be a little unwell. Yesterday I was going upstairs for a book, and suddenly on the stairs, imagine, I suddenly sat down on a step and cried... God knows why, and afterwards for a long time tears kept coming to my eyes... What can that mean? And yet I feel well...

BELYAEV. It is growing. You are growing up. It happens. That is why your eyes looked rather swollen yesterday evening.

VERA. And did you notice?

BELYAEV. Of course.

VERA. You notice everything.

BELYAEV. Well, no... not everything.

VERA. (Thoughtfully.) Alexei Nikolayich...

BELYAEV. What?

VERA. (After a pause.) What was I going to ask you? I have forgotten, really, what I wanted to ask.

BELYAEV. Are you so absent-minded?

VERA. No... but... oh yes! This is what I wanted to ask. You told me, I think, that you have a sister?

BELYAEV. I have.

VERA. Tell me, am I like her?

BELYAEV. Oh no. You are much prettier than she.

VERA. How can that be! Your sister... I should like to be in her place.

BELYAEV. What? You would like to be in our little house now?

VERA. That is not what I meant to say... Is your house small?

BELYAEV. Very small... not like this.

VERA. And what is the use of so many rooms?

BELYAEV. What is the use? In time you will find out what rooms are for.

VERA. In time... When?

BELYAEV. When you are a mistress of a house yourself...

VERA. (Thoughtfully.) Do you think so?

BELYAEV. You will see. (After a pause.) Well then, shall I go and fetch Kolya, Vera Alexandrovna... eh?

VERA. Why do you not call me Verochka?

BELYAEV. And can you call me Alexei?...

VERA. Why not... (Suddenly starting.) Oh!

BELYAEV. What is it?

VERA. (In a low voice.) Natalya Petrovna is coming here.

BELYAEV. (Also in a low voice.) Where?

VERA. (Pointing with her head.) There, on the path, with Mikhail Alexandrych.

BELYAEV. (Rising.) Let us go to Kolya... He must have finished his lesson by now.

VERA. Let us go... or I am afraid she will scold me...

(They both rise and go quickly out to the left. Katya hides in the raspberry patch again. Natalya Petrovna and Rakitin enter from the right.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Stopping.) Is that not Mr. Belyaev going away with Verochka?

RAKITIN. Yes, it is they...

NATALYA PETROVNA. They seem to be running away from us.

RAKITIN. Perhaps.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) However, I do not think it right for Verochka... to be alone with a young man in the garden like that... Of course she is a child; but all the same it is improper... I shall tell her.

RAKITIN. How old is she?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Seventeen! She is already seventeen... And today it is hot. I am tired. Let us sit down.

(They both sit down on the bench on which Vera and Belyaev had been sitting.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Has Shpigelsky gone?

RAKITIN. He has.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You were wrong not to keep him. I do not know why that man took it into his head to become a district doctor... He is very amusing. He makes me laugh.

RAKITIN. And I imagined you were not in a mood for laughter today.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Why did you think so?

RAKITIN. Just so!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Because I dislike everything sentimental today? Oh yes! I warn you, today nothing in the world can touch me. But that does not prevent my laughing, on the contrary. Besides, I needed to have a word with Shpigelsky.

RAKITIN. May I know about what?

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, you may not. You know in any case everything I think, everything I do... It is tedious.

RAKITIN. Forgive me... I did not suppose...

NATALYA PETROVNA. I should like to hide something from you, at least.

RAKITIN. For heaven's sake! From your words one might conclude that I know everything...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him.) And do you not?

RAKITIN. You are pleased to laugh at me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. So you really do not know all that is going on in me? In that case I do not congratulate you. What? A man watches me from morning to night...

RAKITIN. What is this, a reproach?

NATALYA PETROVNA. A reproach? (After a pause.) No, now I truly see: you are not penetrating.

RAKITIN. Perhaps... but since I watch you from morning to night, allow me to share with you one observation...

NATALYA PETROVNA. About me? By all means.

RAKITIN. You will not be angry with me?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh no! I should like to be, but no.

RAKITIN. For some time, Natalya Petrovna, you have been in a sort of constantly irritated state, and this irritation in you is involuntary, inward: you seem to be struggling with yourself, as if bewildered. Before my trip to the Krinitsyns' I did not notice it; it is recent in you.

(Natalya Petrovna draws lines on the ground in front of her with her parasol.)

You sometimes sigh so deeply... as a tired man sighs, a very tired man who cannot manage to rest.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And what do you conclude from that, Mr. Observer?

RAKITIN. Nothing... But it troubles me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I thank you humbly for your concern.

RAKITIN. And besides...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some impatience.) Please, let us change the subject.

(Silence.)

RAKITIN. Are you not intending to go out anywhere today?

NATALYA PETROVNA. No.

RAKITIN. Why not? The weather is fine.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Laziness.

(Silence.)

Tell me... you know Bolshintsov, don't you?

RAKITIN. Our neighbour, Afanasy Ivanych?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes.

RAKITIN. What a question! Not further back than the day before yesterday he and I played preference at your house.

NATALYA PETROVNA. What sort of man is he, I want to know.

RAKITIN. Bolshintsov?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, yes, Bolshintsov.

RAKITIN. That, I confess, I should never have expected!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Impatiently.) What should you never have expected?

RAKITIN. That you would ever ask about Bolshintsov! A stupid, fat, heavy man, but otherwise nothing bad can be said of him.

NATALYA PETROVNA. He is not at all so stupid and so heavy as you think.

RAKITIN. Perhaps. I confess I have not studied this gentleman very attentively.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Ironically.) You have not observed him?

RAKITIN. (Smiling with an effort.) And what put it into your head...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Just so!

(Silence again.)

RAKITIN. Look, Natalya Petrovna, how fine this dark green oak is against the dark blue sky. It is all flooded with the rays of the sun, and what powerful colours... What indestructible life and strength is in it, especially when you compare it with that young birch there... She seems quite ready to vanish in the radiance, her tiny leaves shine with a sort of liquid brilliance, as though they were melting, and yet she too is beautiful...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Do you know what, Rakitin? I noticed it long ago... You feel the so-called beauties of nature very delicately and speak of them very gracefully, very cleverly... so gracefully, so cleverly that I imagine nature must be inexpressibly grateful to you for your exquisitely happy phrases; you court her like a perfumed marquis in red heels courting a pretty peasant girl... The only trouble is this: it sometimes seems to me that she could never understand or appreciate your subtle remarks, just as the peasant girl would not understand the marquis's court compliments; nature is much simpler, even coarser, than you suppose, for she is, thank God, healthy... Birches do not melt or faint like nervous ladies.

RAKITIN. What a tirade! Nature is healthy... that is, in other words, I am an unhealthy creature.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You are not the only unhealthy creature; neither of us is very well.

RAKITIN. Oh, I know too this way of saying to another, in the most inoffensive manner, the most unpleasant things... Instead of telling him, for example, straight in his face: you, my dear fellow, are a fool, one need only remark to him with a good-natured smile: we are both of us fools, after all.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Are you offended? Come, what nonsense! I only wanted to say that we both... the word “unhealthy” does not please you... that we both are old, very old.

RAKITIN. Why old? I do not think so about myself.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, but listen; here we are sitting now... perhaps on this very bench, a quarter of an hour before us, there sat... two truly young creatures.

RAKITIN. Belyaev and Verochka? Of course they are younger than we... there are a few years between us, that is all... But that does not make us old people yet.

NATALYA PETROVNA. The difference between us is not in years alone...

RAKITIN. Ah! I understand... You envy their... naivety, their freshness, innocence... in a word, their foolishness...

NATALYA PETROVNA. You think so? Ah! You think they are foolish? You think everyone foolish today, I see. No, you do not understand me. And besides... foolish! What does that matter! What is good in intelligence when it does not amuse? There is nothing more wearisome than a joyless mind.

RAKITIN. Hm. Why will you not speak directly, without hints? I do not amuse you, that is what you mean to say... Why make intelligence in general suffer for poor sinful me?

NATALYA PETROVNA. That is not it at all...

(Katya comes out of the raspberry patch.)

What is this, have you picked raspberries, Katya?

KATYA. Yes, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Show me...

(Katya goes up to her.)

Lovely raspberries! How scarlet... and your cheeks are still scarlet.

(Katya smiles and lowers her eyes.)

Well, go.

(Katya goes out.)

RAKITIN. Here is another young creature to your taste.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Of course. (She rises.)

RAKITIN. Where are you going?

NATALYA PETROVNA. First, I want to see what Verochka is doing... it is time for her to come home... and second, I confess, our conversation does not please me somehow. We had better stop for a while our reasonings about nature and youth.

RAKITIN. Perhaps you would like to walk alone?

NATALYA PETROVNA. To tell the truth, yes. We shall see each other soon... But we part friends, do we not? (She holds out her hand to him.)

RAKITIN. (Rising.) Of course! (He presses her hand.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Goodbye.

(She opens her parasol and goes out to the left.)

RAKITIN. (Walking up and down for some time.) What is the matter with her? (After a pause.) Just so! A caprice. A caprice? I never noticed it in her before. On the contrary, I know no woman more even in her manner. What is the reason?... (He walks again and suddenly stops.) Ah, how absurd are people who have one thought in the head, one aim, one occupation in life... As I have, for example. She was right: from morning to night you watch trifles and become trifling yourself... All that is so; but without her I cannot live, in her presence I am more than happy; this feeling cannot be called happiness, I belong wholly to her, to part from her would be, without any exaggeration, exactly the same as parting from life. What is the matter with her? What does this inner anxiety mean, this involuntary bitterness of speech? Am I beginning to bore her? Hm. (He sits down.) I have never deceived myself; I know very well how she loves me; but I hoped that this quiet feeling in time... I hoped! Have I the right, do I dare to hope? I confess, my position is rather ridiculous... almost contemptible. (After a pause.) Well, why such words? She is an honest woman, and I am no Lovelace. (With a bitter smile.) Unfortunately. (Rising quickly.) Well, enough! Out of my head with all this nonsense! (Pacing.) What a beautiful day it is today! (After a pause.) How neatly she stung me... My “exquisitely happy” phrases... She is very clever, especially when she is out of humour. And what is this sudden worship of simplicity and innocence?... This Russian tutor... She often speaks to me of him. I confess I see nothing special in him. Simply a student, like all students. Can it be that she... It cannot be! She is out of humour... does not know herself what she wants, and so she scratches me. Children beat their nurse, too... What a flattering comparison! But I must not hinder her. When this attack of melancholy restlessness passes, she herself will be the first to laugh at this lanky fledgling, this fresh youth... Your explanation is not bad, Mikhail Alexandrych, my friend, but is it true? God knows! We shall see. It has happened to you before more than once, my dearest fellow, after long wrestling with yourself, suddenly to give up all suppositions and calculations, quietly fold your little hands and humbly wait to see what will happen. And meanwhile confess, you yourself are quite uncomfortable and bitter... Such is your trade...

(Looking round.) Ah! and here he is himself, our unaffected youth... Opportunely come... I have not yet had one proper talk with him. Let us see what sort of man he is.

(Belyaev enters from the left.)

Ah, Alexei Nikolayich! And you have come out for a walk in the fresh air?

BELYAEV. Yes.

RAKITIN. That is, to tell the truth, the air is not quite fresh today; the heat is terrible, but here, under these limes, in the shade, it is fairly bearable. (After a pause.) Have you seen Natalya Petrovna?

BELYAEV. I have just met her... She has gone into the house with Vera Alexandrovna.

RAKITIN. But was it not you I saw here with Vera Alexandrovna, half an hour ago?

BELYAEV. Yes... I was walking with her.

RAKITIN. Ah! (He takes his arm.) Well, how do you like life in the country?

BELYAEV. I like the country. One trouble: the shooting is poor here.

RAKITIN. And are you a sportsman?

BELYAEV. Yes... And you?

RAKITIN. I? No; I confess I am a poor shot. I am too lazy.

BELYAEV. I am lazy too... only not at walking.

RAKITIN. Ah! What, are you lazy at reading?

BELYAEV. No, I like reading. I am lazy at long work; especially to occupy myself with one and the same subject for long makes me lazy.

RAKITIN. (Smiling.) Well, and for example, conversing with ladies?

BELYAEV. Oh! You are laughing at me... Ladies I fear most of all.

RAKITIN. (With some embarrassment.) What put that into your head... why should I laugh at you?

BELYAEV. Just so... what does it matter! (After a pause.) Tell me, where can one get gunpowder here?

RAKITIN. In town, I think; it is sold there under the name of poppy-seed. Do you need good?

BELYAEV. No; even rifle powder will do. It is not for shooting, it is for making fireworks.

RAKITIN. Ah! You know how...

BELYAEV. I do. I have already chosen the place: beyond the pond. I heard that in a week it is Natalya Petrovna's name-day; so it would be just the thing.

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna will be very pleased by such attention on your part... She likes you, Alexei Nikolayich, I may tell you.

BELYAEV. I am very flattered... Oh, by the way, Mikhailo Alexandrych, you take a journal, I think. Could you let me have it to read?

RAKITIN. By all means, with pleasure... There are some good verses in it...

BELYAEV. I am not fond of verses.

RAKITIN. Why not?

BELYAEV. Just so. Comic verses seem to me strained, and there are few of them besides; and sentimental verses... I do not know... somehow one does not believe them.

RAKITIN. You prefer tales?

BELYAEV. Yes, good tales I like... but critical articles, those take hold of me.

RAKITIN. How so?

BELYAEV. A warm-blooded man writes them...

RAKITIN. And do you yourself not occupy yourself with literature?

BELYAEV. Oh no! What is the use of writing if God has given one no talent. Only to make people laugh. And besides, there is this wonder, explain it to me, if you please: some person, even clever it seems, as soon as he takes up the pen, he is quite unbearable. No, how could we write; God grant that we understand what is written!

RAKITIN. Do you know what, Alexei Nikolayich? Not many young men have as much good sense as you.

BELYAEV. I thank you humbly for the compliment. (After a pause.) I chose the place for the fireworks beyond the pond because I can make Roman candles that burn on water...

RAKITIN. That must be very beautiful... Forgive me, Alexei Nikolayich, but allow me to ask you... Do you know French?

BELYAEV. No. I translated a novel by Paul de Kock, “The Milkmaid of Montfermeil,” perhaps you have heard of it, for fifty paper roubles; but I do not know a word of French. Imagine, “quatre-vingt-dix” I translated: four twenty-ten... Need, you know, forced me. And it is a pity. I should like to know French. But the cursed laziness. I should like to read George Sand in French. And the pronunciation... how is one to cope with the pronunciation? an, on, en, en... A misery!

RAKITIN. Well, that trouble can still be mended...

BELYAEV. Allow me to ask, what time is it?

RAKITIN. (Looking at his watch.) Half past one.

BELYAEV. Why does Lizaveta Bogdanovna keep Kolya so long at the pianoforte... He must be dying to run about now.

RAKITIN. (Kindly.) But one must study too, Alexei Nikolayich...

BELYAEV. (With a sigh.) It is not for you to say that, Mikhail Alexandrych, nor for me to hear... Of course not everyone can be such a scapegrace as I am.

RAKITIN. Come, come...

BELYAEV. Oh, I know that well enough...

RAKITIN. And I, on the contrary, also know, and for certain, that precisely what you consider a fault in yourself, this ease of yours, your freedom, that is exactly what pleases.

BELYAEV. Whom, for example?

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, for one.

BELYAEV. Natalya Petrovna? With her I do not feel free, as you put it.

RAKITIN. Ah! Really?

BELYAEV. And finally, for pity's sake, Mikhail Alexandrych, is not education the first thing in a man? It is easy for you to talk... I really do not understand you... (Suddenly stopping.) What is that? Did a corncrake call in the garden? (He wants to go.)

RAKITIN. Perhaps... but where are you going?

BELYAEV. For my gun...

(He goes towards the wings on the left; Natalya Petrovna comes out to meet him.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Seeing him, suddenly smiling.) Where are you going, Alexei Nikolayich?

BELYAEV. I...

RAKITIN. For his gun... He heard a corncrake in the garden...

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, please do not shoot in the garden... Let the poor bird live... Besides, you may frighten grandmother.

BELYAEV. Very good, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Laughing.) Oh, Alexei Nikolayich, are you not ashamed? “Very good, ma'am”... what sort of phrase is that? How can one... speak so? But wait; Mikhail Alexandrych and I shall take your education in hand... Yes, yes... We have already talked about you more than once. There is a conspiracy against you, I warn you. You will allow me to take your education in hand, will you not?

BELYAEV. For pity's sake... I...

NATALYA PETROVNA. First of all, do not be shy, it does not suit you at all. Yes, we shall take you in hand. (Pointing to Rakitin.) For he and I are old folk, and you are a young man... Is it not so? See how well it will all go. You will busy yourself with Kolya, and I... and we with you.

BELYAEV. I shall be very grateful to you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. That is right. What were you talking about here with Mikhailo Alexandrych?

RAKITIN. (Smiling.) He was telling me how he translated a French book without knowing a word of French.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! Well, we shall teach you French too. And by the way, what have you done with your kite?

BELYAEV. I took it home. It seemed to me that you... disliked it...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some embarrassment.) Why did that seem so to you? Because I... because I took Verochka home? No, that... No, you were mistaken. (Briskly.) However, do you know what? Kolya must have finished his lesson now. Let us go and fetch him, Verochka, the kite, shall we? And all go off together to the meadow. Eh?

BELYAEV. With pleasure, Natalya Petrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Splendid. Well, come, come. (She holds out her hand to him.) Do take my hand, how awkward you are! Come... quickly.

(They both go quickly out to the left.)

RAKITIN. (Looking after them.) What animation, what gaiety... I never saw such an expression on her face. And what a sudden change! (After a pause.) Souvent femme varie... But I... I am decidedly not to her taste today. That is clear. (After a pause.) Well! We shall see what comes next. (Slowly.) Can it be... (He waves his hand.) It cannot be!... But that smile, that cordial, soft, bright look... Ah, God forbid that I should know the torments of jealousy, especially senseless jealousy! (Suddenly looking round.) Well, well, well... what chance brings you?

(Shpigelsky and Bolshintsov enter from the left. Rakitin goes to meet them.)

Good day, gentlemen... I confess, Shpigelsky, I did not expect you today... (He shakes their hands.)

SHPIGELSKY. Nor did I myself, sir... I myself did not imagine... Well, I drove over to him (pointing to Bolshintsov), and he was already sitting in his carriage, on his way here. Well, I turned my shafts back at once and came back together with him.

RAKITIN. Well, welcome.

BOLSHINTSOV. I really was intending...

SHPIGELSKY. (Cutting short his speech.) The servants told us the family were all in the garden... At least there was no one in the drawing-room...

RAKITIN. But did you not meet Natalya Petrovna?

SHPIGELSKY. When?

RAKITIN. Just now.

SHPIGELSKY. No. We did not come here straight from the house. Afanasy Ivanych wanted to see whether there are mushrooms in the grove.

BOLSHINTSOV. (Bewildered.) I...

SHPIGELSKY. Why, yes, we know you are a great hunter of boletus mushrooms. So Natalya Petrovna has gone home? Well? We can go back too.

BOLSHINTSOV. Of course.

RAKITIN. Yes, she has gone home to call everyone out for a walk... They are going to fly a kite, I think.

SHPIGELSKY. Ah! And splendid. In such weather one ought to walk.

RAKITIN. You may stay here... I shall go and tell her that you have come.

SHPIGELSKY. Why should you trouble... For heaven's sake, Mikhailo Alexandrych...

RAKITIN. No... I have to in any case...

SHPIGELSKY. Ah! Well, in that case we do not detain you... No ceremony, you know...

RAKITIN. Goodbye, gentlemen. (He goes out to the left.)

SHPIGELSKY. Goodbye. (To Bolshintsov.) Well then, Afanasy Ivanych...

BOLSHINTSOV. (Interrupting him.) What put it into your head, Ignaty Ilyich, to say that about mushrooms... I am amazed; what mushrooms?

SHPIGELSKY. And I suppose, in your opinion, I ought to have said that my Afanasy Ivanych has lost courage, did not want to come straight in, and asked to go round by the side?

BOLSHINTSOV. That is so... but still, mushrooms... I do not know, perhaps I am mistaken...

SHPIGELSKY. You certainly are mistaken, my friend. Rather think of this. Here we have come... it is done as you wished. Mind now! Do not disgrace yourself.

BOLSHINTSOV. Yes, Ignaty Ilyich, but you... you told me, that is... I should like to know positively what the answer is...

SHPIGELSKY. My most honoured Afanasy Ivanych! From your village to here it is fifteen versts and more; at every verst you put the same question to me at least three times... Is that really not enough for you? Well, listen: but I am indulging you for the last time. This is what Natalya Petrovna said to me: “I...”

BOLSHINTSOV. (Nodding.) Yes.

SHPIGELSKY. (Vexed.) Yes... Well, what “yes”? I have not told you anything yet... “I,” she says, “know Mr. Bolshintsov little, but he seems to me a good man; on the other hand, I have not the slightest intention of forcing Verochka; and therefore let him visit us, and if he deserves...”

BOLSHINTSOV. Deserves? She said: deserves?

SHPIGELSKY. “If he deserves her favour, Anna Semyonovna and I will not stand in the way...”

BOLSHINTSOV. “Will not stand in the way”? She actually said so? Will not stand in the way?

SHPIGELSKY. Yes, yes, yes. What a strange man you are! “Will not stand in the way of their happiness.”

BOLSHINTSOV. Hm.

SHPIGELSKY. “Their happiness.” Yes; but note, Afanasy Ivanych, what the task is now: you must now convince Vera Alexandrovna herself that marriage with you is indeed happiness for her; you must win her favour.

BOLSHINTSOV. (Blinking.) Yes, yes, win it... exactly; I agree with you.

SHPIGELSKY. You insisted that I bring you here today without fail... Well, let us see how you will act.

BOLSHINTSOV. Act? Yes, yes, I must act, I must win her, exactly. Only this, Ignaty Ilyich... Allow me to confess to you, as to my best friend, one weakness of mine: I wished, as you say, that you should bring me here today...

SHPIGELSKY. Not wished, but demanded, relentlessly demanded.

BOLSHINTSOV. Well, yes, let us say so... I agree with you. But you see: at home... at home I was ready, it seemed, for anything; and now timidity overcomes me.

SHPIGELSKY. But why are you timid?

BOLSHINTSOV. (Looking at him from under his brows.) Risk, sir.

SHPIGELSKY. What?

BOLSHINTSOV. Risk, sir. A great risk, sir. I must confess to you, Ignaty Ilyich, as...

SHPIGELSKY. (Interrupting.) As to your best friend... we know, we know... Go on?

BOLSHINTSOV. Exactly so, sir, I agree with you. I must confess to you, Ignaty Ilyich, that I... I have had in general little, so to speak, intercourse with ladies, with the female sex in general; I, Ignaty Ilyich, confess to you frankly, simply cannot think of what one can talk about with a female person, and moreover tête-à-tête... especially with a young lady.

SHPIGELSKY. You astonish me. I, on the contrary, do not know what one cannot talk about with a female person, especially with a young lady, and especially tête-à-tête.

BOLSHINTSOV. Well, but you... For heaven's sake, how can I compare with you? That is why I should like to have recourse to you, Ignaty Ilyich. They say in these matters the hardest thing is to begin, so couldn't you... for the opening of the conversation... give me a little word, some pleasant remark, for instance, a comment... and then I shall go on. Then I shall somehow manage by myself.

SHPIGELSKY. A little word I shall not give you, Afanasy Ivanych, because no little word would serve you in the least... but advice I can give you, if you like.

BOLSHINTSOV. Do me the favour, my dear fellow... And as for my gratitude... you know...

SHPIGELSKY. Enough, enough; am I bargaining with you?

BOLSHINTSOV. (Lowering his voice.) Be easy about the three.

SHPIGELSKY. Enough, at last! Now see here, Afanasy Ivanych... You are undoubtedly an excellent man in all respects...

(Bolshintsov bows slightly.)

a man of splendid qualities...

BOLSHINTSOV. Oh, I beg you!

SHPIGELSKY. Besides, you have, I think, three hundred souls?

BOLSHINTSOV. Three hundred and twenty, sir.

SHPIGELSKY. Unmortgaged?

BOLSHINTSOV. I owe not a kopeck.

SHPIGELSKY. Well, you see. I told you that you are an excellent man and a match for anyone. But you yourself say that you have had little intercourse with ladies...

BOLSHINTSOV. (With a sigh.) Exactly so, sir. I may say, Ignaty Ilyich, I have shunned the female sex from childhood.

SHPIGELSKY. (With a sigh.) Well, you see. In a husband that is no vice, on the contrary; but all the same in some cases, for instance, at the first declaration of love, one must be able to say at least something... Is it not so?

BOLSHINTSOV. I quite agree with you.

SHPIGELSKY. Otherwise Vera Alexandrovna may think that you feel unwell, and nothing more. Besides, your figure, though also in all respects presentable, offers nothing that, you know, strikes the eye, and nowadays that is required.

BOLSHINTSOV. (With a sigh.) Nowadays that is required.

SHPIGELSKY. Young ladies at least like it. Well, and then your years, finally... in a word, we two are not going to win by gallantry. So you need not think of pleasant little words. That is a poor prop. But you have another prop, much firmer and more reliable, namely your qualities, most honoured Afanasy Ivanych, and your three hundred and twenty souls. In your place I would simply say to Vera Alexandrovna...

BOLSHINTSOV. Tête-à-tête?

SHPIGELSKY. Oh, certainly tête-à-tête! “Vera Alexandrovna!”

(By the movements of Bolshintsov's lips it is clear that he is repeating every word after Shpigelsky in a whisper.)

“I love you and ask for your hand. I am a kind, plain, quiet man, and not poor: you will be completely free with me; I shall do my utmost to please you. And be good enough to make inquiries about me, be good enough to pay me rather more attention than so far, and give me whatever answer you wish and whenever you wish. I am ready to wait, and I shall even count it a pleasure.”

BOLSHINTSOV. (Pronouncing the last word loudly.) A pleasure. So, so, so... I agree with you. Only this, Ignaty Ilyich: you were pleased, I think, to use the word “quiet”... that is to say, I am a quiet man...

SHPIGELSKY. Well, and are you not a quiet man?

BOLSHINTSOV. Ye-es... but still, it seems to me... Will it be proper, Ignaty Ilyich? Would it not be better to say, for example...

SHPIGELSKY. For example?

BOLSHINTSOV. For example... for example... (After a pause.) However, one may perhaps say “quiet” too.

SHPIGELSKY. Eh, Afanasy Ivanych, take my advice; the more simply you express yourself, the fewer ornaments you introduce into your speech, the better things will go, believe me. And above all, do not insist, do not insist, Afanasy Ivanych. Vera Alexandrovna is still very young; you may frighten her... Give her time to think over your proposal properly. Yes! one more thing... I almost forgot; you have allowed me to give you advice... It happens to you sometimes, my dear Afanasy Ivanych, to say “fruct” and “tayl”... Well, why not... it may be done... but you know, the words “fruit” and “tail” are somehow more usual; they have, so to speak, come more into use. And another thing, if I remember, you once in my presence called a hospitable landowner a “bonjiban”; you said, “what a bonjiban he is!” A good word too, of course, but unfortunately it means nothing. You know I am myself none too strong in the French dialect, but I know that much. Avoid eloquence, and I guarantee you success. (Looking round.) And here they all come this way, by the way.

(Bolshintsov wants to slip away.)

Where are you going? Back to the mushrooms?

(Bolshintsov smiles, blushes and stays.)

SHPIGELSKY. The main thing is not to be timid!

BOLSHINTSOV. (Hurriedly.) But Vera Alexandrovna knows nothing yet?

SHPIGELSKY. Of course not!

BOLSHINTSOV. However, I rely on you...

(Bolshintsov blows his nose. From the left enter Natalya Petrovna, Vera, Belyaev with the kite, Kolya, and behind them Rakitin and Lizaveta Bogdanovna.)

(Natalya Petrovna is in very good spirits.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Bolshintsov and Shpigelsky.) Ah, good day, gentlemen; good day, Shpigelsky; I did not expect you today, but I am always glad to see you. Good day, Afanasy Ivanych.

(Bolshintsov bows with some confusion.)

SHPIGELSKY. (To Natalya Petrovna, pointing to Bolshintsov.) This gentleman insisted on bringing me here...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Laughing.) I am much obliged to him... But do you need to be forced to come to us?

SHPIGELSKY. For heaven's sake! But... It was only this morning... From here... For heaven's sake...

NATALYA PETROVNA. You have got tangled, tangled, Mr. Diplomat!

SHPIGELSKY. Natalya Petrovna, it is very pleasant for me to see you in such, as far as I can observe, a cheerful frame of mind.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! You think it necessary to observe that... But does this happen to me so rarely?

SHPIGELSKY. Oh, for heaven's sake, no... but...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Monsieur le diplomate, you are getting more and more tangled.

KOLYA. (Who has been fidgeting impatiently around Belyaev and Vera all this time.) But maman, when shall we fly the kite?

NATALYA PETROVNA. When you like... Alexei Nikolayich, and you, Verochka, let us go to the meadow... (Turning to the others.) You, gentlemen, I think, cannot be much interested in this. Lizaveta Bogdanovna, and you, Rakitin, I entrust our good Afanasy Ivanych to you.

RAKITIN. But why, Natalya Petrovna, do you think it would not interest us?

NATALYA PETROVNA. You are clever people... To you it must seem a childish prank... However, as you like. We do not prevent you from following us... (To Belyaev and Verochka.) Let us go.

(Natalya Petrovna, Vera, Belyaev and Kolya go out to the right.)

SHPIGELSKY. (Looking with some surprise at Rakitin, to Bolshintsov.) Our good Afanasy Ivanych, give your arm to Lizaveta Bogdanovna.

BOLSHINTSOV. (Hurriedly.) With great pleasure... (He takes Lizaveta Bogdanovna's arm.)

SHPIGELSKY. (To Rakitin.) And we shall go with you, if you permit, Mikhailo Alexandrych. (He takes his arm.) See how they run along the avenue. Let us go and see how they fly the kite, clever people though we are... Afanasy Ivanych, will you kindly go ahead?

BOLSHINTSOV. (Walking, to Lizaveta Bogdanovna.) The weather today is, one may say, very pleasant, ma'am.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Mincingly.) Oh, very!

SHPIGELSKY. (To Rakitin.) And I need to speak to you, Mikhailo Alexandrych...

(Rakitin suddenly laughs.)

SHPIGELSKY. What is it?

RAKITIN. Nothing... I am amused that we have ended up in the rear guard.

SHPIGELSKY. The vanguard, you know, very easily becomes the rear guard... It is all a matter of changing direction.

(They all go out to the right.)

## III · The Confession

The same set as in the first act. Rakitin and Shpigelsky enter from the door of the ballroom.

SHPIGELSKY. Well then, Mikhail Alexandrych, help me, do me the favour.

RAKITIN. But how can I help you, Ignaty Ilyich?

SHPIGELSKY. How? For heaven's sake. Put yourself in my position, Mikhailo Alexandrych. Strictly speaking, I am only an outside party in this affair, of course; I acted, one may say, chiefly out of a wish to oblige... My good heart will ruin me yet!

RAKITIN. (Laughing.) Well, you are still far from ruin.

SHPIGELSKY. (Laughing too.) That is not known yet, but my position is indeed awkward. I brought Bolshintsov here at Natalya Petrovna's wish, and communicated her answer to him with her own permission, and now, on one side, they pout at me as if I had done something foolish, and on the other, Bolshintsov gives me no peace. They avoid him, they do not speak to me...

RAKITIN. And what made you take up this affair, Ignaty Ilyich? Bolshintsov, between ourselves, is simply stupid.

SHPIGELSKY. There you are: between ourselves! What news you have been pleased to tell me! Since when do only clever men marry? If anywhere, in marriage fools ought not to be deprived of their bread. You say I took up this affair... Not at all. This is how it came about: a friend asks me to put in a word for him... What of it? Was I to refuse him? I am a kind man: I do not know how to refuse. I carry out my friend's commission; they answer me: “Humbly thank you; do not trouble yourself any more...” I understand and trouble no more. Then suddenly they themselves propose to me and encourage me, so to speak... I obey; and they are indignant with me. What am I guilty of here?

RAKITIN. But who says you are guilty... I am only astonished at one thing: why are you so busy about it?

SHPIGELSKY. Why... why... The man gives me no peace.

RAKITIN. Come, come...

SHPIGELSKY. Besides, he is an old friend of mine.

RAKITIN. (With an incredulous smile.) Yes! Well, that is another matter.

SHPIGELSKY. (Smiling too.) However, I do not want to be crafty with you... One cannot deceive you. Well, yes... he promised me... one of my trace-horses has gone lame, so he promised me...

RAKITIN. Another trace-horse?

SHPIGELSKY. No, to confess, a whole troika.

RAKITIN. You should have said so long ago!

SHPIGELSKY. (Quickly.) But please do not think... I would never have agreed to be an intermediary in such a matter, it is completely contrary to my nature,

(Rakitin smiles.)

if I did not know Bolshintsov for the most honourable of men... However, even now I wish for one thing only: let them answer me decisively, yes or no?

RAKITIN. Has it really gone as far as that?

SHPIGELSKY. What do you imagine?... It is not a question of marriage, but of permission to visit, to call...

RAKITIN. But who can forbid that?

SHPIGELSKY. What people you are... forbid! Of course, for anyone else... but Bolshintsov is a timid man, an innocent soul, straight out of the golden age of Astraea, he only lacks a rag to suck... He has little confidence in himself, he needs a little encouragement. Besides, his intentions are of the noblest.

RAKITIN. And the horses are good too.

SHPIGELSKY. And the horses are good. (He takes snuff and offers Rakitin his snuffbox.) Will you not?

RAKITIN. No, thank you.

SHPIGELSKY. So, so it is, Mikhailo Alexandrych. I do not want to deceive you, you see. And why? The matter is as plain as the palm of your hand. A man of honest principles, with means, quiet... If he suits, good. If he does not suit, well, then say so.

RAKITIN. All this is excellent, let us say; but what am I in it? I truly do not see how I can be of use.

SHPIGELSKY. Eh, Mikhailo Alexandrych! Do we not know that Natalya Petrovna respects you very much and even sometimes listens to you?... Truly, Mikhailo Alexandrych (embracing him from the side), be a friend, put in a word...

RAKITIN. And do you think he would be a good husband for Verochka?

SHPIGELSKY. (Assuming a serious air.) I am convinced of it. You do not believe it... You will see. For in marriage, you know yourself, the chief thing is a solid character! And who is more solid than Bolshintsov! (Looking round.) And here, it seems, comes Natalya Petrovna herself... My dear sir, father, benefactor! Two chestnuts on the traces, a bay in the shafts! Do take the trouble!

RAKITIN. (Smiling.) Well, well, all right...

SHPIGELSKY. Mind now, I rely on you... (He escapes into the ballroom.)

RAKITIN. (Looking after him.) What a sly fellow this doctor is! Verochka... and Bolshintsov! However, why not? There are worse marriages. I shall carry out his commission, and then it is not my affair!

(He turns round; Natalya Petrovna comes out of the study and, seeing him, stops.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Hesitantly.) Is that... you... I thought you were in the garden...

RAKITIN. You seem displeased...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him.) Oh, come now! (She goes to the front of the stage.) Are you here alone?

RAKITIN. Shpigelsky has just left here.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Slightly knitting her brows.) Ah! This district Talleyrand... What was he telling you? Is he still hanging about here?

RAKITIN. This district Talleyrand, as you call him, is apparently out of favour with you today... though yesterday, it seems...

NATALYA PETROVNA. He is ridiculous; he is amusing indeed; but... he meddles in what is not his business... That is unpleasant. Besides, for all his servility, he is very insolent and importunate... He is a great cynic.

RAKITIN. (Going up to her.) Yesterday you did not speak of him so...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Perhaps. (Briskly.) So what was he telling you?

RAKITIN. He was telling me... about Bolshintsov...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! About that stupid man?

RAKITIN. And of him too you spoke differently yesterday.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With a forced smile.) Yesterday is not today.

RAKITIN. For everyone... but apparently not for me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Lowering her eyes.) How so?

RAKITIN. For me today is the same as yesterday.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Holding out her hand to him.) I understand your reproach, but you are mistaken. Yesterday I would not have admitted that I am to blame before you...

(Rakitin tries to stop her.)

Do not contradict me... I know, and you know, what I want to say... and today I admit it. I have thought over much today... Believe me, Michel, whatever foolish thoughts occupy me, whatever I say, whatever I do, I rely on no one as I rely on you. (Lowering her voice.) I love no one... as I love you...

(A short silence.)

You do not believe me?

RAKITIN. I believe you... but you seem sad today... what is the matter with you?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Not listening to him and continuing.) Only I have become convinced of one thing, Rakitin: in no case can one answer for oneself, and for nothing can one vouch. We often do not understand our past... how are we to answer for the future! One cannot put chains on the future.

RAKITIN. That is true.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a long silence.) Listen, I want to be frank with you, perhaps I shall grieve you a little... but I know that my concealment would grieve you still more. I confess to you, Michel, this young student... this Belyaev has made a rather strong impression on me...

RAKITIN. (In a low voice.) I knew it.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! You noticed it? How long ago?

RAKITIN. Since yesterday.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah!

RAKITIN. The day before yesterday already, remember, I spoke to you about the change that had taken place in you... I did not then know what to attribute it to. But yesterday, after our conversation... and in that meadow... If you could have seen yourself! I did not recognize you; you seemed to have become another woman. You laughed, you jumped, you frolicked like a little girl, your eyes shone, your cheeks glowed, and with what trusting curiosity, with what joyful attention you looked at him, how you smiled... (Glancing at her.) Even now your face lights up at the mere memory... (He turns away.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, Rakitin, for God's sake, do not turn away from me... Listen: why exaggerate? This man has infected me with his youth, and that is all. I myself was never young, Michel, from my very childhood until now... You know my whole life... From lack of habit all this went to my head like wine, but, I know, it will pass as quickly as it came... It is not even worth speaking about. (After a pause.) Only do not turn away from me, do not take your hand from me... Help me...

RAKITIN. (In a low voice.) Help you... a cruel word! (Aloud.) You yourself do not know, Natalya Petrovna, what is happening to you. You are sure it is not worth speaking about, and you ask for help... Evidently you feel you need it!

NATALYA PETROVNA. That is... yes... I turn to you as to a friend.

RAKITIN. (Bitterly.) Yes... I, Natalya Petrovna, am ready to justify your trust... but allow me to collect myself a little.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Collect yourself? But is some... unpleasantness threatening you? Has something changed?

RAKITIN. (Bitterly.) Oh no! Everything is as before.

NATALYA PETROVNA. But what are you thinking, Michel? Can you really suppose...

RAKITIN. I suppose nothing.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Can you really despise me so much...

RAKITIN. Stop, for God's sake. Let us rather talk of Bolshintsov. The doctor is waiting for an answer about Verochka, you know.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Sadly.) You are angry with me.

RAKITIN. I? Oh no. But I am sorry for you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Truly, it is even annoying. Michel, are you not ashamed...

(Rakitin is silent. She shrugs her shoulders and continues with vexation.)

You say the doctor is waiting for an answer? And who asked him to interfere...

RAKITIN. He assured me that you yourself...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him.) Perhaps, perhaps... Although I think I told him nothing positive... Besides, I may change my intentions. And finally, good heavens, what does it matter! Shpigelsky busies himself with business of every kind; in his trade he cannot succeed in everything.

RAKITIN. He only wishes to know what the answer is...

NATALYA PETROVNA. What answer... (After a pause.) Michel, come, give me your hand... why this indifferent look, this cold politeness?... What am I guilty of? Think, is it my fault? I came to you in the hope of hearing good advice, I did not hesitate for a moment, I did not think of hiding from you, and you... I see I was wrong to be frank with you... It would not even have entered your head... You suspected nothing, you deceived me. And now you think God knows what.

RAKITIN. I? For heaven's sake!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Give me your hand...

(He does not move; she continues, somewhat offended.)

You turn away from me decidedly? Well, all the worse for you. However, I do not blame you... (Bitterly.) You are jealous!

RAKITIN. I have no right to be jealous, Natalya Petrovna... For heaven's sake, what are you saying?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) As you like. And as to Bolshintsov, I have not yet spoken to Verochka.

RAKITIN. I could send her to you at once.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Why at once!... However, as you like.

RAKITIN. (Making for the study door.) So you wish me to send her?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Michel, for the last time... You told me just now that you are sorry for me... So this is how sorry you are for me! Can it be...

RAKITIN. (Coldly.) Do you wish it?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With vexation.) Yes.

(Rakitin goes into the study. Natalya Petrovna remains motionless for some time, then sits down, takes a book from the table, opens it and lets it fall on her lap.)

And he too! What is this, then? He... and he! And I still relied on him. And Arkady? My God! I did not even remember him! (Straightening up.) I see it is time to put an end to all this...

(Vera comes in from the study.)

Yes... it is time.

VERA. (Timidly.) You asked for me, Natalya Petrovna?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Quickly looking round.) Ah! Verochka! Yes, I asked for you.

VERA. (Going up to her.) Are you well?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I? Yes. Why?

VERA. It seemed to me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, it is nothing. I am a little warm... that is all. Sit down.

(Vera sits down.)

Listen, Vera; you are not busy with anything just now?

VERA. No, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I ask because I need to talk with you... to talk seriously. Look, my dear, until now you have been a child; but you are seventeen; you are clever... It is time for you to think of your future. You know I love you like a daughter; my house will always be your home... but all the same in the eyes of other people you are an orphan; you are not rich. In time you may grow tired of always living among strangers; listen, would you like to be the mistress, the full mistress, in a house of your own?

VERA. (Slowly.) I do not understand you, Natalya Petrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) Your hand has been asked for.

(Vera looks at Natalya Petrovna in astonishment.)

You did not expect this; I confess, to me too it seems rather strange. You are still so young... I need not tell you that I have not the slightest intention of forcing you... in my opinion you are still too young to marry; I only thought it my duty to tell you...

(Vera suddenly covers her face with her hands.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. What is this? You are crying? (She takes her by the hand.) You are trembling all over?... Can you be afraid of me, Vera?

VERA. (Dully.) I am in your power, Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Taking Vera's hands from her face.) Vera, are you not ashamed to cry? Are you not ashamed to say that you are in my power? What do you take me for? I speak to you as to a daughter, and you...

(Vera kisses her hands.)

Eh? You are in my power? Then be so good as to laugh at once... I order you...

(Vera smiles through her tears.)

That is right. (She puts one arm round her and draws her to her.) Vera, my child, be with me as you would be with your mother, or no, better, imagine that I am your elder sister, and let us talk together about all these wonders... Will you?

VERA. I am ready.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, listen... Move a little closer. So. First: since I am your sister, let us suppose, I have no need to assure you that you are at home here: such eyes are at home everywhere. So it must not even enter your head that you are a burden to anyone in the world or that anyone wants to get rid of you... Do you hear? But one fine day your sister comes to you and says: imagine, Vera, you have been proposed for... Eh? What will you answer her? That you are still very young, that you do not even think of marrying?

VERA. Yes, ma'am.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Do not say “yes, ma'am” to me. Does one say “yes, ma'am” to sisters?

VERA. (Smiling.) Well... yes.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Your sister will agree with you, the suitor will be refused, and that is the end of it. But if the suitor is a good man, with means, if he is ready to wait, if he asks only for permission to see you from time to time, in the hope of pleasing you in time.

VERA. And who is this suitor?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! You are curious. You do not guess?

VERA. No.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You saw him today.

(Vera blushes all over.)

He is, it is true, not very handsome and not very young... Bolshintsov.

VERA. Afanasy Ivanych?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes... Afanasy Ivanych.

VERA. (She looks at Natalya for some time, suddenly begins to laugh and stops.) You are not joking?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Smiling.) No... but, I see, Bolshintsov has nothing more to do here. If you had cried at his name, he might still have hoped, but you burst out laughing. One thing is left for him: to go home with God's blessing.

VERA. Forgive me... but truly I did not expect it... Do people still marry at his age?

NATALYA PETROVNA. What do you think? How old is he? He is not fifty. He is in his prime.

VERA. Perhaps... but he has such a strange face...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, let us not speak of him any more. He is dead and buried... God be with him! However, it is understandable: a girl of your age cannot like such a man as Bolshintsov... You all want to marry for love, not for reason, do you not?

VERA. Yes, Natalya Petrovna, you... did you not also marry Arkady Sergeich for love?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) Of course, for love. (After another pause, squeezing Vera's hand.) Yes, Vera... I called you a girl just now... but girls are right.

(Vera lowers her eyes.)

So then, this matter is settled. Bolshintsov is dismissed. I confess, I myself should not have liked to see his puffy old face beside your fresh little face, though he is a very good man after all. Now do you see how in vain you were afraid of me? How quickly everything was arranged!... (Reproachfully.) Truly, you behaved to me as if I were your benefactress! You know how I hate that word...

VERA. (Embracing her.) Forgive me, Natalya Petrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. That is right. Are you really not afraid of me?

VERA. No. I love you; I am not afraid of you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, thank you. So now we are great friends and shall hide nothing from each other. Well, and if I were to ask you: Verochka, tell me in my ear: is it only because he is much older than you and no beauty that you do not want to marry Bolshintsov?

VERA. But is that not enough, Natalya Petrovna?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I do not dispute it... but is there no other reason?

VERA. I do not know him at all...

NATALYA PETROVNA. That is so; but you are not answering my question.

VERA. There is no other reason.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Really? In that case I should advise you to think it over. It is hard to fall in love with Bolshintsov, I know... but he is, I repeat, a good man. Now if you loved someone else... well, then it would be another matter. But your heart is still silent, is it not?

VERA. (Timidly.) How, ma'am?

NATALYA PETROVNA. You love no one yet?

VERA. I love you... Kolya; I love Anna Semyonovna too.

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, I am not speaking of that love, you do not understand me... For example, among the young men whom you could see here or on visits, does really not one of them please you?

VERA. No... some please me, but...

NATALYA PETROVNA. For example, I noticed that at the Krinitsyns' party you danced three times with that tall officer... what is his name?

VERA. With an officer?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, the one with such big moustaches.

VERA. Oh, that one!... No, I do not like him.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, and Shalansky?

VERA. Shalansky is a good man; but he... I think he has no thought for me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. What do you mean?

VERA. He... he seems to think more about Liza Velskaya.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Glancing at her.) Ah!... you noticed that?...

(Silence.)

Well, and Rakitin?

VERA. I like Mikhail Alexandrovich very much...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, like a brother. And by the way, Belyaev...

VERA. (Blushing.) Alexei Nikolayich? I like Alexei Nikolayich.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Watching Vera.) Yes, he is a good man. Only he is so shy with everyone...

VERA. (Innocently.) No... He is not shy with me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah!

VERA. He talks to me. Perhaps it seems so to you because he... He is afraid of you. He has not yet had time to know you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And how do you know that he is afraid of me?

VERA. He told me.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! He told you... He is more frank with you, then, than with others?

VERA. I do not know how he is with others, but with me... perhaps because we are both orphans. Besides... in his eyes I am... a child.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You think so? However, I too like him very much. He must have a kind heart.

VERA. Oh, a most kind one! If you only knew... everyone in the house loves him. He is so gentle. He talks to everybody, ready to help everybody. The day before yesterday he carried a beggar woman in his arms from the high road to the hospital... Once he plucked a flower for me from such a cliff that I even shut my eyes with fear; I was sure he would fall and be dashed to pieces... but he is so agile! You could see yourself yesterday in the meadow how agile he is.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, that is true.

VERA. Do you remember, when he ran after the kite, what a ditch he jumped over? And it is all nothing to him.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And did he really pluck a flower for you from a dangerous place? He must love you.

VERA. (After a pause.) And he is always cheerful... always in good spirits...

NATALYA PETROVNA. That is strange, however. Why in my presence he...

VERA. (Interrupting her.) But I tell you, he does not know you. But wait, I shall tell him... I shall tell him there is nothing to be afraid of in you, shall I? that you are so kind...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Laughing with an effort.) Thank you.

VERA. You will see... And he listens to me, even though I am younger than he.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I did not know you were on such friendly terms with him... Be careful, though, Vera. He is, of course, an excellent young man... but you know, at your age... it is not fitting. People might think... I already remarked it to you yesterday, do you remember? in the garden.

(Vera lowers her eyes.)

On the other hand, I do not wish to hinder your inclinations either, I am too sure of you and of him... but all the same... Do not be angry with me, my dear, for my pedantry... it is our old people's business to weary the young with lectures. However, I am saying all this in vain; for, is it not so, you like him, and nothing more?

VERA. (Timidly raising her eyes.) He...

NATALYA PETROVNA. There, you look at me as before again? Does one look so at a sister? Vera, listen, bend down to me... (Caressing her.) What if your sister, your real sister, asked you in your ear now: Verochka, do you truly love no one? Eh? What would you answer her?

(Vera glances hesitantly at Natalya Petrovna.)

These little eyes want to tell me something...

(Vera suddenly presses her face against her breast. Natalya Petrovna turns pale and, after a pause, continues.)

You love? Tell me, do you love?

VERA. (Without raising her head.) Oh! I do not know myself what is the matter with me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Poor child! You are in love...

(Vera presses still closer to Natalya Petrovna's breast.)

You are in love... and he? Vera, does he?

VERA. (Still not raising her head.) Why do you ask me... I do not know... Perhaps... I do not know, I do not know...

(Natalya Petrovna shudders and remains motionless. Vera raises her head and suddenly notices the change in her face.)

Natalya Petrovna, what is the matter?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Coming to herself.) With me... nothing... What?... nothing.

VERA. You are so pale, Natalya Petrovna... What is it? Let me ring... (She rises.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. No, no... do not ring... It is nothing... It will pass. There, it has already passed.

VERA. Let me at least call someone...

NATALYA PETROVNA. On the contrary... I... I want to be alone. Leave me; do you hear? We shall talk again. Go.

VERA. You are not angry with me, Natalya Petrovna?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I? What for? Not in the least. On the contrary, I am grateful to you for your confidence... Only leave me, please, now.

(Vera tries to take her hand, but Natalya Petrovna turns away as though not noticing Vera's movement.)

VERA. (With tears in her eyes.) Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Leave me, I beg you.

(Vera goes slowly into the study.)

(Alone, she remains motionless for some time.) Now everything is clear to me... These children love each other... (She stops and passes her hand over her face.) Well? So much the better... God give them happiness! (Laughing.) And I... I could think... (She stops again.) She soon let it slip... I confess, I had not suspected... I confess, this news has struck me... But wait, all is not yet over. My God... what am I saying? What is the matter with me? I do not recognize myself. To what have I come? (After a pause.) What am I doing? I want to marry off a poor girl... to an old man!... I send the doctor... he guesses, hints... Arkady, Rakitin... And I... (She shudders and suddenly raises her head.) But what is this, finally? Am I jealous of Vera? I... am I in love with him? (After a pause.) And do you still doubt? You are in love, wretched woman! How it happened... I do not know. As though I had been given poison... Suddenly everything is shattered, scattered, swept away... He is afraid of me... Everyone is afraid of me. What is there in me for him?... What use to him is a creature like me? He is young, and she is young. And I! (Bitterly.) How should he appreciate me? They are both fools, as Rakitin says... Oh! how I hate that clever man! And Arkady, my trusting, kind Arkady! My God, my God! Send me death! (She rises.) However, it seems to me I am losing my mind. Why exaggerate! Well, yes... I am struck... it is a novelty to me, it is the first time... I... yes! the first time! I am in love for the first time now! (She sits down again.) He must go away. Yes. And Rakitin too. It is time for me to come to my senses. I allowed myself to step aside one pace, and see! See where I have come. And what did I like in him? (She grows thoughtful.) So here it is, this dreadful feeling... Arkady! Yes, I shall go into his arms, I shall beg him to forgive me, to protect me, to save me. He... and nobody else! All the others are strangers to me and must remain strangers... But is there not... is there not another means? That girl is a child. She may have been mistaken. It is all childishness, after all... Why do I... I shall explain myself with him, I shall ask him... (Reproachfully.) Ah, ah? You still hope? You still want to hope? And what do I hope for! My God, do not let me despise myself! (She bows her head on her hands.)

(Rakitin comes in from the study, pale and anxious.)

RAKITIN. (Going up to Natalya Petrovna.) Natalya Petrovna... (She does not stir. Aside.) What can have happened to her with Vera? (Aloud.) Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Raising her head.) Who is it? Ah! you.

RAKITIN. Vera Alexandrovna told me that you were unwell... I...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Turning away.) I am well... What put it into her head...

RAKITIN. No, Natalya Petrovna; you are unwell, look at yourself.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, perhaps... but what is it to you? What do you want? Why have you come?

RAKITIN. (In a moved voice.) I shall tell you why I came. I came to ask your forgiveness. Half an hour ago I was unspeakably stupid and rude to you... Forgive me. You see, Natalya Petrovna, however modest a man's wishes and... and hopes may be, it is hard for him not to lose himself, if only for a moment, when they are suddenly torn from him; but now I have come to my senses, I have understood my position and my fault and wish for one thing only, your forgiveness. (He sits down quietly beside her.) Look at me... do not turn away either. Before you is your old Rakitin, your friend, a man who asks nothing but permission to serve you, as you said, as a support... Do not deprive me of your trust, dispose of me and forget that I once... Forget everything that may have offended you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Who has been staring at the floor all the time.) Yes, yes... (She stops.) Oh, forgive me, Rakitin, I did not hear anything of what you said to me.

RAKITIN. (Sadly.) I said... I asked your forgiveness, Natalya Petrovna. I asked whether you would allow me to remain your friend.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Slowly turning to him and laying both hands on his shoulders.) Rakitin, tell me, what is the matter with me?

RAKITIN. (After a pause.) You are in love.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Slowly repeating after him.) I am in love... But this is madness, Rakitin. It is impossible. Can it come so suddenly... You say I am in love... (She falls silent.)

RAKITIN. Yes, you are in love, poor woman... Do not deceive yourself.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Not looking at him.) What am I to do now?

RAKITIN. I am ready to tell you, Natalya Petrovna, if you promise me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him and still not looking at him.) You know that that girl, Vera, loves him... They are both in love with each other.

RAKITIN. In that case one more reason...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting him again.) I have long suspected it, but she herself has just confessed everything... just now.

RAKITIN. (In a low voice, as if to himself.) Poor woman!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Passing her hand over her face.) Well, however... it is time to come to my senses. You wanted to tell me something, I think... Advise me, for God's sake, Rakitin, what I am to do...

RAKITIN. I am ready to advise you, Natalya Petrovna, but only on one condition.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Speak, what is it?

RAKITIN. Promise me that you will not suspect my intentions. Tell me that you believe in my disinterested wish to help you; help me too. Your trust will give me strength, or else let me keep silent.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Speak, speak.

RAKITIN. You do not doubt me?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Speak.

RAKITIN. Well then, listen: he must go away.

(Natalya Petrovna looks at him in silence.)

Yes, he must go away. I shall not speak to you of... your husband, of your duty. In my mouth these words are... out of place... But these children love each other. You yourself have just told me so; imagine yourself now between them... You will be lost!

NATALYA PETROVNA. He must go away... (After a pause.) And you? Will you stay?

RAKITIN. (With embarrassment.) I?... I?... (After a pause.) I must go away too. For your peace, for your happiness, for Verochka's happiness, he and I, we both must go away for ever.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Rakitin... I have come to such a pass that I... I was almost ready to give this poor girl, an orphan entrusted to me by my mother, in marriage to a stupid, ridiculous old man!... My courage failed me, Rakitin; the words died on my lips when she burst out laughing at my proposal... but I plotted with that doctor, I let him smile significantly; I endured those smiles, his civilities, his hints... Oh, I feel I am on the brink of a precipice, save me!

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, you see that I was right... (She is silent; he continues hurriedly.) He must go away... we both must go away... There is no other salvation.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Dejectedly.) But what is there to live for afterwards?

RAKITIN. My God, has it really come to this... Natalya Petrovna, you will recover, believe me... It will all pass. What do you mean, what to live for?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, yes, what is there to live for, when everyone leaves me?

RAKITIN. But... your family...

(Natalya Petrovna lowers her eyes.)

Listen, if you wish, after his departure I can stay a few days more... in order to...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Gloomily.) Ah! I understand you. You count on habit, on old friendship... You hope that I shall come to myself, that I shall come back to you; is it not so? I understand you.

RAKITIN. (Blushing.) Natalya Petrovna! Why do you insult me?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Bitterly.) I understand you... but you deceive yourself.

RAKITIN. What? After your promises, after I, for you, for you alone, for your happiness, for your position in society, finally...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah! Since when are you so concerned about that? Why did you never speak to me of it before?

RAKITIN. (Rising.) Natalya Petrovna, I shall leave here today, at once, and you will never see me again... (He wants to go.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Stretching out her hands to him.) Michel, forgive me; I do not know what I am saying... You see the state I am in. Forgive me.

RAKITIN. (Quickly returning to her and taking her by the hands.) Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah, Michel, it is unspeakably hard for me... (She leans on his shoulder and presses her handkerchief to her eyes.) Help me, I am lost without you...

(At this moment the door of the ballroom opens, and Islayev and Anna Semyonovna come in.)

ISLAYEV. (Loudly.) I have always been of the opinion...

(He stops in astonishment at the sight of Rakitin and Natalya Petrovna.)

(Natalya Petrovna looks round and quickly goes out. Rakitin remains where he is, extremely embarrassed.)

ISLAYEV. (To Rakitin.) What does this mean? What sort of scene is this?

RAKITIN. Nothing... it is...

ISLAYEV. Is Natalya Petrovna unwell, then?

RAKITIN. No... but...

ISLAYEV. And why did she suddenly run away? What were you talking about with her? She seemed to be crying... You were comforting her... What is it?

RAKITIN. Truly, nothing.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. But how can it be nothing, Mikhail Alexandrych? (After a pause.) I shall go and see... (She wants to go into the study.)

RAKITIN. (Stopping her.) No, you had better leave her in peace now, I beg you.

ISLAYEV. But what does it all mean? Tell me, at last!

RAKITIN. Nothing, I swear to you... Listen, I promise you both to explain everything today. I give you my word. But now, please, if you trust me, do not ask me anything, and do not disturb Natalya Petrovna.

ISLAYEV. Very well... only it is astonishing. This never happened to Natasha before. There is something extraordinary in it.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. The main thing is, what could have made Natasha cry? And why did she go away!... Are we strangers?

RAKITIN. What are you saying! How can you! But listen: to tell the truth, we have not finished our conversation... I must ask you... both... leave us alone for a while.

ISLAYEV. Indeed! So there is a secret between you?

RAKITIN. A secret... but you will learn it.

ISLAYEV. (After thinking.) Come, mamma... let us leave them. Let them finish their mysterious conversation.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. But...

ISLAYEV. Come, come. You hear, he promises to explain everything.

RAKITIN. You may be at ease...

ISLAYEV. (Coldly.) Oh, I am perfectly at ease! (To Anna Semyonovna.) Come.

(They both go out.)

RAKITIN. (Looking after them and quickly going to the door of the study.) Natalya Petrovna... Natalya Petrovna, come out, I beg you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Coming out of the study. She is very pale.) What did they say?

RAKITIN. Nothing, be calm... They were indeed a little surprised. Your husband thought you were unwell... He noticed your agitation... Sit down; you can hardly stand...

(Natalya Petrovna sits down.)

I told him... I asked him not to disturb you... to leave us alone.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And did he agree?

RAKITIN. Yes. I confess, I had to promise him that tomorrow I would explain everything... Why did you go away?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Bitterly.) Why!... But what will you say?

RAKITIN. I... I shall think of something. That is not the point now... We must take advantage of this respite. You see, it cannot go on like this... You are not able to bear such anxieties... they are unworthy of you... I myself... But that is not the question. Only be firm, and I will do the rest! Listen, you agree with me, don't you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. About what?

RAKITIN. About the necessity of... our departure? Do you agree? In that case there is no time to lose. If you allow me, I shall speak to Belyaev myself at once... He is a noble man, he will understand...

NATALYA PETROVNA. You want to speak to him? You? But what can you say to him?

RAKITIN. (With embarrassment.) I...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) Rakitin, listen, does it not seem to you that we are both like madmen?... I was frightened, I frightened you, and perhaps all over trifles.

RAKITIN. How?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Truly. What is the matter with us? It seems not long ago that everything was so quiet, so peaceful in this house... and suddenly... where did it all come from! Truly, we have all gone out of our minds. Enough, we have played the fool long enough... Let us live as before... And you will have nothing to explain to Arkady; I shall tell him our pranks myself, and we shall laugh over them together. I do not need an intermediary between myself and my husband!

RAKITIN. Natalya Petrovna, now you frighten me. You smile and are pale as death... Remember at least what you told me a quarter of an hour ago...

NATALYA PETROVNA. One says many things! However, I see what it is... You yourself raise this storm... so that at least you may not drown alone.

RAKITIN. Again, again suspicion, again reproach, Natalya Petrovna... God be with you... but you torment me. Or do you repent of your frankness?

NATALYA PETROVNA. I repent of nothing.

RAKITIN. Then how am I to understand you?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Briskly.) Rakitin, if you say one word from me or about me to Belyaev, I shall never forgive you.

RAKITIN. Ah! So that is it!... Be easy, Natalya Petrovna. Not only shall I say nothing to Mr. Belyaev, I shall not even say goodbye to him when I leave here. I have no intention of forcing my services on anyone.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some embarrassment.) But perhaps you think I have changed my mind about... his departure?

RAKITIN. I think nothing.

NATALYA PETROVNA. On the contrary, I am so convinced of the necessity, as you say, of his departure that I intend to dismiss him myself. (After a pause.) Yes; I shall dismiss him myself.

RAKITIN. You?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, I. And at once. I ask you to send him to me.

RAKITIN. What? At once?

NATALYA PETROVNA. At once. I ask you this, Rakitin. You see, I am calm now. Besides, no one will interrupt us now. We must take advantage of it... I shall be very grateful to you. I shall question him.

RAKITIN. But he will tell you nothing, for heaven's sake. He himself confessed to me that he feels awkward in your presence.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Suspiciously.) Ah! You have already spoken with him about me?

(Rakitin shrugs his shoulders.)

Well, forgive me, forgive me, Michel, and send him to me. You will see, I shall dismiss him, and all will be over. It will all pass and be forgotten like a bad dream. Please send him to me. I must absolutely talk with him once and for all. You will be pleased with me. Please.

RAKITIN. (Who has not taken his eyes off her all the time, coldly and sadly.) Very well. Your wish shall be fulfilled. (He goes to the door of the ballroom.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After him.) Thank you, Michel.

RAKITIN. (Turning round.) Oh, at least do not thank me... (He quickly goes out into the ballroom.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Alone, after a pause.) He is a noble man... But did I ever really love him? (She rises.) He is right. That one must go away. But how to dismiss him! I only want to know whether he really likes that girl. Perhaps it is all nonsense. How could I fall into such agitation... what was the use of all these outpourings? Well, now there is nothing to be done. I want to know what he will tell me. But he must go away... Absolutely... absolutely. Perhaps he will not want to answer me... He is afraid of me, after all... Well? So much the better. I need not talk with him much... (She puts her hand to her forehead.) And my head aches. Should I not put it off till tomorrow? Truly. Today I keep feeling that I am being watched... To what have I come! No, it is better to finish at once... One more effort, the last, and I am free!... Oh yes! I thirst for freedom and peace.

(Belyaev comes in from the ballroom.)

It is he...

BELYAEV. (Going up to her.) Natalya Petrovna, Mikhailo Alexandrych told me that you wished to see me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some effort.) Yes, indeed... I need to have an... explanation with you.

BELYAEV. An explanation?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Not looking at him.) Yes... an explanation. (After a pause.) Allow me to tell you, Alexei Nikolayich, that I... I am displeased with you.

BELYAEV. May I know the reason?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Hear me out... I... truly, I do not know where to begin. However, I must warn you that my displeasure does not arise from any neglect... in your duties... On the contrary, your manner with Kolya pleases me.

BELYAEV. Then what can it be?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Glancing at him.) You are needlessly alarmed... Your fault is not so great. You are young; you have probably never lived in another's house. You could not foresee...

BELYAEV. But, Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. You wish to know, at last, what the matter is? I understand your impatience. So I must tell you that Verochka... (looking at him) Verochka has confessed everything to me.

BELYAEV. (In astonishment.) Vera Alexandrovna? What could Vera Alexandrovna confess to you? And what have I to do with it?

NATALYA PETROVNA. And you really do not know what she could have confessed? You do not guess?

BELYAEV. I? Not in the least.

NATALYA PETROVNA. In that case forgive me. If you truly do not guess, I must ask your pardon. I thought... I was mistaken. But allow me to observe that I do not believe you. I understand what makes you speak so... I greatly respect your modesty.

BELYAEV. I decidedly do not understand you, Natalya Petrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Really? Do you mean to assure me that you have not noticed this child Vera's liking for you?

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna's liking for me? I do not even know what to say to you... For heaven's sake. I think I have always been with Vera Alexandrovna as...

NATALYA PETROVNA. As with everyone, is it not so? (After a short pause.) However that may be, whether you truly do not know it or pretend not to know, the matter is this: this girl loves you. She herself confessed it to me. Now I ask you, as an honourable man, what do you intend to do?

BELYAEV. (In confusion.) What do I intend to do?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Folding her arms.) Yes.

BELYAEV. All this is so unexpected, Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause.) Alexei Nikolayich, I see... I have gone about this affair badly. You do not understand me. You think I am angry with you... but I am... only... a little agitated. And that is very natural. Calm yourself. Let us sit down.

(They both sit down.)

I shall be frank with you, Alexei Nikolayich, be a little more trusting with me too. Truly, you shun me needlessly. Vera loves you... of course, you are not to blame for it; I am ready to suppose you are not to blame... But you see, Alexei Nikolayich, she is an orphan, my ward: I am answerable for her, for her future, for her happiness. She is still young, and, I am sure, the feeling you have inspired in her may soon disappear... at her age people do not love for long. But you understand that it was my duty to warn you. To play with fire is dangerous all the same... and I do not doubt that you, knowing now her feeling for you, will change your manner with her, will avoid meetings, walks in the garden... Is it not so? I may rely on you... With another I should have been afraid to explain myself so directly.

BELYAEV. Natalya Petrovna, believe me, I know how to appreciate...

NATALYA PETROVNA. I tell you that I do not doubt you... Besides, all this will remain a secret between us.

BELYAEV. I confess, Natalya Petrovna, all that you have said to me seems to me so strange... of course I dare not disbelieve you, but...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Listen, Alexei Nikolayich. All that I have said to you now... I said on the supposition that on your side there is nothing... (She breaks off.) because in the opposite case... of course, I know you but little yet, but I know you well enough already not to see any reason to oppose your intentions. You are not rich... but you are young, you have a future, and when two people love each other... I, I repeat, considered it my duty to warn you, as an honest man, about the consequences of your acquaintance with Vera, but if you...

BELYAEV. (Bewildered.) I truly do not know, Natalya Petrovna, what you mean...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Hastily.) Oh, believe me, I do not require a confession from you, I do not need it... I shall understand from your conduct what the case is... (Glancing at him.) However, I must tell you that it seemed to Vera that you too are not quite indifferent to her.

BELYAEV. (After a pause, rising.) Natalya Petrovna, I see I cannot remain in your house.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Flushing.) You might, it seems, have waited for me to dismiss you myself... (She rises.)

BELYAEV. You have been frank with me... Allow me too to be frank with you. I do not love Vera Alexandrovna; at least I do not love her as you suppose.

NATALYA PETROVNA. But did I... (She stops.)

BELYAEV. And if I pleased Vera Alexandrovna, if it seemed to her that I too, as you say, am not indifferent to her, I do not want to deceive her; I shall tell her everything myself, the whole truth. But after such an explanation, you will understand yourself, Natalya Petrovna, it will be hard for me to remain here: my position would be too awkward. I shall not tell you how hard it is for me to leave your house... I have nothing else to do. I shall always remember you with gratitude... Allow me to withdraw... I shall still have the honour of taking leave of you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With feigned indifference.) As you wish... but I confess I did not expect this... I did not at all intend to explain myself with you for that purpose... I only wanted to warn you... Vera is still a child... Perhaps I have attached too much importance to all this. I do not see the necessity of your departure. However, as you wish.

BELYAEV. Natalya Petrovna... truly, it is impossible for me to stay here any longer.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You evidently part from us very easily!

BELYAEV. No, Natalya Petrovna, not easily.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I am not accustomed to keep people against their will... but I confess it is very unpleasant to me.

BELYAEV. (After some hesitation.) Natalya Petrovna... I should not like to cause you the slightest unpleasantness... I stay.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Ah!... (After a pause.) I did not expect that you would change your decision so quickly... I am grateful to you, but... Allow me to think. Perhaps you are right; perhaps you really must go. I shall think it over, I shall let you know... Will you allow me to leave you in uncertainty until this evening?

BELYAEV. I am ready to wait as long as you like. (He bows and wants to go.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. You promise me...

BELYAEV. (Stopping.) What?

NATALYA PETROVNA. You wanted, I think, to explain yourself to Vera... I do not know whether that would be proper. However, I shall let you know my decision. I begin to think that you really must go away. Goodbye.

(Belyaev bows a second time and goes into the ballroom. Natalya Petrovna looks after him.)

I am calm! He does not love her... (She paces the room.) So, instead of dismissing him, I myself have kept him? He stays... But what shall I say to Rakitin? What have I done? (After a pause.) And what right had I to divulge this poor girl's love?... How? I myself drew the confession from her... half a confession, and then I myself so pitilessly, so roughly... (She covers her face with her hands.) Perhaps he was beginning to love her... By what right did I trample this flower in the bud... And is it really trampled? Perhaps he deceived me... I too wanted to deceive him!... Oh no! He is too noble for that... He is not like me! And why was I in such a hurry? Why did I blab everything at once? (Sighing.) One says many things! If I could have foreseen... How I schemed, how I lied before him... and he! How boldly and freely he spoke... I bowed before him... He is a man! I did not know him yet... He must go away. If he stays... I feel I shall come to the point of losing all respect for myself... He must go away, or I am lost! I shall write to him before he has time to see Vera... He must go away! (She quickly goes into the study.)

## IV · The Stone Hall

The scene is a large empty entrance hall. The walls are bare, the floor uneven and stone; six whitewashed, flaking brick columns, three on each side, support the ceiling. On the left, two open windows and a door to the garden. On the right, a door to the corridor leading to the main house; straight ahead, an iron door to the storeroom. Beside the first column on the right, a green garden bench; in one corner, several spades, watering cans and pots. It is evening. Red rays of the sun fall through the windows onto the floor.

KATYA. (Coming in by the door on the right, going briskly to the window and looking into the garden for some time.) No, he is not to be seen. And they told me he had gone to the greenhouse. He has not come out of there yet, then. Well, I shall wait till he passes by. He has no other way... (She sighs and leans against the window.) They say he is leaving. (She sighs again.) How shall we manage without him... Poor young lady! How she begged me... Well, why not oblige her? Let her speak with him at the last. How warm it is today! And I think it is beginning to drizzle... (She looks out of the window again and suddenly steps back.) Are they not coming here?... They are coming here, indeed. Oh, dear me...

(She tries to run away but has not yet reached the corridor door when Shpigelsky comes in from the garden with Lizaveta Bogdanovna.)

(Katya hides behind a column.)

SHPIGELSKY. (Shaking out his hat.) We can wait out the rain here. It will soon pass.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Very well.

SHPIGELSKY. (Looking round.) What building is this? A storeroom, is it?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Pointing to the iron door.) No, the storeroom is there. This hall, they say, was added by Arkady Sergeich's father when he came back from abroad.

SHPIGELSKY. Ah! I see what it is: Venice, my dear sir. (He sits down on the bench.) Let us sit down.

(Lizaveta Bogdanovna sits down.)

And admit, Lizaveta Bogdanovna, that this rain came at a bad moment. It interrupted our explanations at the most sensitive place.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Lowering her eyes.) Ignaty Ilyich...

SHPIGELSKY. But nobody prevents us from resuming our conversation... By the way, you say Anna Semyonovna is out of humour today?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Yes, out of humour. She even dined in her room.

SHPIGELSKY. Is that so! What a misfortune, to think of it!

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. This morning she found Natalya Petrovna in tears... with Mikhail Alexandrych... He is, of course, one of the family, but all the same... However, Mikhailo Alexandrych promised to explain everything.

SHPIGELSKY. Ah! Well, she is troubling herself in vain. Mikhailo Alexandrych, in my opinion, never was a dangerous man, and now less than ever.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Why?

SHPIGELSKY. Just so. He talks too cleverly. In some it comes out as a rash, but in these clever fellows it all comes out on the tongue, in chatter. You, Lizaveta Bogdanovna, need never fear chatterboxes: they are not dangerous; but those who say little, and have a fancy in them, and plenty of temperament and a broad back of the neck, those are the dangerous ones.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (After a pause.) Tell me, is Natalya Petrovna really unwell?

SHPIGELSKY. As unwell as you and I.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. She ate nothing at dinner.

SHPIGELSKY. It is not only illness that takes away the appetite.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Did you dine at Bolshintsov's?

SHPIGELSKY. Yes, at his house... I drove over to see him. And I came back only for your sake, upon my word.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Well, come now. And do you know what, Ignaty Ilyich? Natalya Petrovna is angry with you about something... She spoke of you at table not quite favourably.

SHPIGELSKY. Really? Evidently the ladies do not like it when our brother has seeing eyes. Do as they wish, help them, and pretend besides that you do not understand them. See what they are! Well, however, we shall see. And Rakitin, I suppose, is hanging his nose?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Yes, he too seems not himself today.

SHPIGELSKY. Hm. And Vera Alexandrovna? Belyaev?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Still, absolutely everyone is out of humour. Truly, I cannot think what is the matter with them all today.

SHPIGELSKY. Know too much, and you will age before your time, Lizaveta Bogdanovna... Well, God be with them. Let us talk rather about our affair. The rain, you see, has not stopped yet... Will you?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Mincingly lowering her eyes.) What are you asking me, Ignaty Ilyich?

SHPIGELSKY. Eh, Lizaveta Bogdanovna, allow me to observe: why should you simper, why suddenly cast down your eyes like that? We are not young people, you and I! These ceremonies, tendernesses, sighs, they do not suit us. Let us speak calmly, to the point, as becomes people of our years. So, this is the question: we like each other... at least I presume that you like me.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Simpering slightly.) Ignaty Ilyich, really...

SHPIGELSKY. Well, yes, yes, all right. For you, as a woman, it is even proper... to put on a little airs, so to speak. So, we like each other. And in other respects too we are well matched. I must say of myself, of course, that I am not a man of high birth: but then you are not of noble origin either. I am not a rich man; otherwise I should have... (He smiles.) But I have a fair practice, my patients do not all die; you, by your own account, have fifteen thousand in ready money; all that, you see, is not bad. Besides, I imagine you are tired of living for ever as a governess, and to fuss with an old lady, to play preference with her and agree with everything, that too must be not very merry. As for me, it is not that I am weary of bachelor life, but I am growing old, and the cooks rob me; so, you see, it all fits together. But here is the difficulty, Lizaveta Bogdanovna: we do not know each other at all, that is, to tell the truth, you do not know me... I know you. Your character is known to me. I shall not say that you have no faults. Being a spinster, you have turned a little sour, but that is no harm. In the hands of a good husband a wife is like soft wax. But I wish you also to know me before the wedding; otherwise you may afterwards blame me... I do not want to deceive you.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (With dignity.) But, Ignaty Ilyich, I think I too have had occasion to learn your character...

SHPIGELSKY. You? Oh, come... That is not a woman's business. You, for example, no doubt think that I am a man of cheerful disposition, a jester, eh?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. It always seemed to me that you are a very amiable man...

SHPIGELSKY. That is just it. You see how easy it is to be mistaken. Because I play the fool before strangers, tell them anecdotes, wait on them, you have already thought that I am really a merry man. If I did not need them, these strangers, I should not even look at them... And indeed wherever one can, without great danger, you know, I make fun of them myself... However, I do not deceive myself; I know that certain gentlemen, who need me at every step, who are bored without me, consider themselves entitled to despise me; but I am not in their debt either. Take Natalya Petrovna... Do you think I do not see through her? (Mimicking her.) “Dear doctor, I truly like you very much... you have such a wicked tongue...” hee, hee, coo away, my dear, coo away. Oh, these ladies! They smile at you and squint their little eyes like that, and loathing is written on their faces... They look down on us, what can you do! I understand why she speaks ill of me today. Truly, these ladies are a wonderful race! Because they wash every day with eau de cologne and speak so carelessly, as if dropping words, pick them up, if you please, they imagine that they cannot be caught by the tail. Not a bit of it! They are mortals like the rest of us sinners!

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Ignaty Ilyich... You astonish me.

SHPIGELSKY. I knew I should astonish you. You see, then, that I am not a merry man at all, perhaps not even very kind... But I too do not wish to pass with you for what I never was. However I may cringe before the gentry, nobody has ever seen me as a buffoon, nobody has ever flicked me on the nose. They are even, I may say, a little afraid of me; they know that I bite. Once, about three years ago, a gentleman, a black-earth sort of fellow, stupidly took it into his head at table to stick a radish in my hair. What do you think? I challenged him that very minute, without heat, you know, in the most polite manner. The black-earth fellow nearly had a stroke from fright; the host made him apologize, the effect was extraordinary!... I knew beforehand, to confess the truth, that he would not fight. There, you see, Lizaveta Bogdanovna, I have a mass of self-love; but life has turned out so. Nor are my talents great... I studied in a sort of way. I am a poor doctor, I have nothing to hide from you, and if you ever fall ill, I shall not be the one to treat you. If I had talents and education, I should have gone off to the capital. Well, for the local inhabitants, of course, no better doctor is needed. As for my temper itself, I must forewarn you, Lizaveta Bogdanovna: at home I am gloomy, silent, exacting; I am not angry when I am pleased and served; I like my habits to be noticed and to be fed well; but otherwise I am neither jealous nor mean, and in my absence you may do whatever you please. Of romantic love between us, you understand, there is no need to speak; but I imagine it is still possible to live with me under one roof... provided I am pleased and no one cries in my presence, that I cannot bear! And I am not fault-finding. There is my confession. Well, what do you say now?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. What can I say to you, Ignaty Ilyich... if you have not blackened yourself on purpose...

SHPIGELSKY. But how have I blackened myself? Do not forget that another man in my place would quite calmly have kept silent about his faults, since you noticed nothing, and after the wedding, my dear, after the wedding it is too late. But I am too proud for that.

(Lizaveta Bogdanovna glances at him.)

Yes, yes, proud... however you may look at me. I do not intend to pretend and lie to my future wife, not for fifteen thousand, not for a hundred thousand; though to a stranger I shall bow very low for a sack of flour. Such is my nature... To a stranger I grin my teeth, and inwardly I think: what a blockhead you are, my dear fellow, what a hook you are biting on; but with you I say what I think. That is, allow me, I do not tell even you all I think; at least I do not deceive you. I must seem to you a great eccentric, truly, but wait, someday I shall tell you my life: you will be amazed that I survived even so far. You too, I dare say, did not eat off gold in childhood, but still, my dear, you cannot imagine what real, inveterate poverty is... However, I shall tell you all that some other time. And now rather think over what I have had the honour to report to you... Consider this little matter carefully, alone, and let me know your decision. You are, as far as I could observe, a sensible woman. You... By the way, how old are you?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. I... I... am thirty.

SHPIGELSKY. (Calmly.) That is not true: you are a full forty.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Flushing.) Not forty at all, but thirty-six.

SHPIGELSKY. Still not thirty. This too, Lizaveta Bogdanovna, you must unlearn... the more so as a married woman at thirty-six is not old at all. You take snuff needlessly too. (Rising.) The rain, it seems, has stopped.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Rising too.) Yes, it has stopped.

SHPIGELSKY. So you will give me an answer in a few days?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. I shall tell you my decision tomorrow.

SHPIGELSKY. That is what I like!... That is wise indeed! Bravo, Lizaveta Bogdanovna! Well, give me your hand. Let us go home.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Giving him her hand.) Let us go.

SHPIGELSKY. And by the way: I have not kissed it... and it seems to be required... Well, this once, here goes! (He kisses her hand.)

(Lizaveta Bogdanovna blushes.)

There. (He makes for the garden door.)

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Stopping.) So you think, Ignaty Ilyich, that Mikhailo Alexandrych really is not a dangerous man?

SHPIGELSKY. I think so.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. Do you know what, Ignaty Ilyich? It seems to me that Natalya Petrovna for some time now... it seems to me that Mr. Belyaev... She pays attention to him... eh? And Verochka too, what do you think? Is it not for that, today...

SHPIGELSKY. (Interrupting her.) I forgot to tell you one more thing, Lizaveta Bogdanovna. I am myself terribly curious, but I cannot bear curious women. That is, I explain: in my opinion a wife ought to be curious and observant (that is even very useful for her husband) only with others... You understand me: with others. However, if you absolutely want to know my opinion about Natalya Petrovna, Vera Alexandrovna, Mr. Belyaev and the local inhabitants in general, listen then, I shall sing you a little song. I have a very bad voice, but do not be hard on me.

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (In surprise.) A little song!

SHPIGELSKY. Listen! First verse: Once there lived at grandmother's a little grey goat, once there lived at grandmother's a little grey goat. Whee, how! Just so! A little grey goat! Whee, how! Just so! A little grey goat!

Second verse: The goat took it into his head to take a walk in the wood, the goat took it into his head to take a walk in the wood. Whee, how! Just so! To walk in the wood! Whee, how! Just so! To walk in the wood!

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. But I truly do not understand...

SHPIGELSKY. Listen! Third verse: The grey wolves ate the little goat, the grey wolves ate the little goat. (Skipping.) Whee, how! Just so! They ate the little goat! Whee, how! Just so! They ate the little goat!

And now let us go. By the way, I need to have a word with Natalya Petrovna. Perhaps she will not bite. If I am not mistaken, she still needs me. Let us go.

(They go out into the garden.)

KATYA. (Cautiously coming out from behind the column.) At last they are gone! What a spiteful doctor... he talked and talked, what he said! And how he sings! I am afraid Alexei Nikolayich may come home in the meantime... And they had to come just here! (She goes to the window.) And Lizaveta Bogdanovna? She will be a doctor's wife... (She laughs.) Look at her... Well, I do not envy her... (Looking out of the window.) How nicely the grass has been washed... how good it smells... It is the bird-cherry smelling so... Ah, here he comes. (After waiting.) Alexei Nikolayich!... Alexei Nikolayich...

BELYAEV. (Offstage.) Who is calling me? Ah, it is you, Katya? (He comes to the window.) What do you want?

KATYA. Come in here... I have something to tell you.

BELYAEV. Ah! Very well. (He moves away from the window and in a minute comes in at the door.) Here I am.

KATYA. Did the rain not wet you?

BELYAEV. No... I was sitting in the greenhouse with Potap... is he your uncle, or what?

KATYA. Yes. He is my uncle.

BELYAEV. How pretty you look today!

(Katya smiles and lowers her eyes. He takes a peach from his pocket.)

Will you have it?

KATYA. (Refusing.) Thank you humbly... eat it yourself.

BELYAEV. And did I refuse when you offered me raspberries yesterday? Take it... I picked it for you... truly.

KATYA. Well, thank you. (She takes the peach.)

BELYAEV. That is better. So what did you want to tell me?

KATYA. The young lady... Vera Alexandrovna asked me... She wishes to see you.

BELYAEV. Ah! Well, I shall go to her at once.

KATYA. No... she will come here herself. She needs to speak with you.

BELYAEV. (With some astonishment.) She wants to come here?

KATYA. Yes. Here, you know... No one comes here. No one can interrupt here... (She sighs.) She loves you very much, Alexei Nikolayich... She is so kind. Shall I go and fetch her now? And will you wait?

BELYAEV. Of course, of course.

KATYA. At once... (She goes and stops.) Alexei Nikolayich, is it true what they say, that you are leaving us?

BELYAEV. I? No... Who told you?

KATYA. So you are not leaving? Well, thank God! (With embarrassment.) We shall be back at once. (She goes out by the door leading to the house.)

BELYAEV. (Remaining motionless for some time.) This is marvellous! Marvels are happening to me. I confess I did not expect all this at all... Vera loves me... Natalya Petrovna knows it... Vera herself confessed everything to her... marvellous! Vera is such a sweet, good child; but... what does this note mean, for instance? (He takes a small scrap of paper from his pocket.) From Natalya Petrovna... in pencil. “Do not go away, do not decide on anything until I have spoken with you.” What does she want to speak to me about? (After a pause.) What foolish thoughts come into my head! I confess all this confuses me exceedingly. If anyone had told me a month ago that I... I... I cannot come to myself after that conversation with Natalya Petrovna. Why does my heart beat so? And now Vera wants to see me... What shall I say to her! At least I shall find out what is the matter... Perhaps Natalya Petrovna is angry with me... But what for? (He looks at the note again.) It is all strange, very strange.

(The door quietly opens. He quickly hides the note. Vera and Katya appear on the threshold. He goes up to them. Vera is very pale, does not raise her eyes and does not move from the spot.)

KATYA. Do not be afraid, miss, go up to him; I shall keep watch... Do not be afraid. (To Belyaev.) Oh, Alexei Nikolayich! (She closes the windows, goes out into the garden and locks the door behind her.)

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna... you wanted to see me. Come here, sit down here. (He takes her by the hand and leads her to the bench.)

(Vera sits down.)

There. (Looking at her in surprise.) You have been crying?

VERA. (Not raising her eyes.) It is nothing... I have come to ask your forgiveness, Alexei Nikolayich.

BELYAEV. For what?

VERA. I heard... you had an unpleasant explanation with Natalya Petrovna... You are leaving... You have been dismissed.

BELYAEV. Who told you that?

VERA. Natalya Petrovna herself... I met her after your explanation with her... She told me that you yourself do not want to stay with us any longer. But I think you were dismissed.

BELYAEV. Tell me, do they know it in the house?

VERA. No... Only Katya... I had to tell her... I wanted to speak with you, to ask your forgiveness. Imagine now how hard it must be for me... For I am the cause of everything, Alexei Nikolayich; I alone am to blame.

BELYAEV. You, Vera Alexandrovna?

VERA. I could never have expected... Natalya Petrovna... However, I excuse her. Excuse me too... This morning I was a foolish child, and now... (She stops.)

BELYAEV. Nothing is decided yet, Vera Alexandrovna... Perhaps I shall stay.

VERA. (Sadly.) You say nothing is decided. Alexei Nikolayich... No, everything is decided, everything is over. See how you are with me now; and remember, only yesterday in the garden... (After a pause.) Ah, I see, Natalya Petrovna has told you everything.

BELYAEV. (In confusion.) Vera Alexandrovna.

VERA. She told you everything, I see it... She wanted to catch me, and I, foolish, threw myself into her net... But she too gave herself away... I am not such a child after all. (Lowering her voice.) Oh no!

BELYAEV. What do you mean?

VERA. (Looking at him.) Alexei Nikolayich, did you really wish to leave us yourself?

BELYAEV. Yes.

VERA. Why?

(Belyaev is silent.)

You do not answer me?

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna, you were not mistaken... Natalya Petrovna told me everything.

VERA. (In a weak voice.) What, for example?

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna... It is truly impossible for me... You understand me.

VERA. Perhaps she told you that I love you?

BELYAEV. (Hesitantly.) Yes.

VERA. (Quickly.) But that is not true...

BELYAEV. (In confusion.) What!...

VERA. (Covering her face with her hands and whispering dully through her fingers.) At least I did not say it to her, I do not remember...

(Raising her head.) Oh, how cruelly she has treated me! And you... is it for this that you want to go away?

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna, judge for yourself...

VERA. (Looking at him.) He does not love me! (She covers her face again.)

BELYAEV. (Sitting down beside her and taking her hands.) Vera Alexandrovna, give me your hand... Listen, there must be no misunderstandings between us. I love you like a sister; I love you because one cannot help loving you. Forgive me if I... I have never been in such a position in my life... I should not like to offend you... I shall not pretend before you; I know that I pleased you, that you came to love me... But judge for yourself what can come of it? I am only twenty, and I have not a penny. Please do not be angry with me. Truly, I do not know what to say to you.

VERA. (Taking her hands from her face and looking at him.) As though I had demanded anything, my God! But why so cruelly, so pitilessly... (She stops.)

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna, I did not wish to grieve you.

VERA. I do not accuse you, Alexei Nikolayich. What are you guilty of! I alone am guilty... and am punished for it! And I do not accuse her either; I know she is a good woman, but she could not overcome herself... She has lost her head.

BELYAEV. (Bewildered.) Lost her head?

VERA. (Turning to him.) Natalya Petrovna loves you, Belyaev.

BELYAEV. How?

VERA. She is in love with you.

BELYAEV. What are you saying?

VERA. I know what I am saying. Today has made me old... I am no longer a child, believe me. She took it into her head to be jealous... of me! (With a bitter smile.) How does that seem to you?

BELYAEV. But that cannot be!

VERA. It cannot be... But why then did she suddenly take it into her head to marry me to that gentleman, what is his name, Bolshintsov? Why did she send the doctor to me, why did she herself persuade me? Oh, I know what I am saying! If you could have seen, Belyaev, how her whole face changed when I told her... Oh, you cannot imagine how cunningly, how slyly she drew this confession from me... Yes, she loves you; it is only too clear...

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna, you are mistaken, I assure you.

VERA. No, I am not mistaken. Believe me: I am not mistaken. If she does not love you, why did she torment me so? What have I done to her? (Bitterly.) Jealousy excuses everything. But what is the use of talking!... And now why does she dismiss you? She thinks that you... that we... Oh, she may be at ease! You may stay! (She covers her face with her hands.)

BELYAEV. She has not dismissed me yet, Vera Alexandrovna... I have already told you that nothing is decided yet...

VERA. (Suddenly raising her head and looking at him.) Really?

BELYAEV. Yes... but why do you look at me so?

VERA. (As if to herself.) Ah! I understand... Yes, yes... she herself still hopes...

(The door from the corridor quickly opens and Natalya Petrovna appears on the threshold. She stops at the sight of Vera and Belyaev.)

BELYAEV. What are you saying?

VERA. Yes, now it is all clear to me... She has come to her senses, she has understood that I am not dangerous to her! and really, what am I? A foolish girl, and she!

BELYAEV. Vera Alexandrovna, how can you think...

VERA. And finally, who knows? Perhaps she is right... perhaps you love her...

BELYAEV. I?

VERA. (Rising.) Yes, you; why do you blush?

BELYAEV. I, Vera Alexandrovna?

VERA. You love her, you can come to love her?... You do not answer my question?

BELYAEV. But for heaven's sake, what do you want me to answer? Vera Alexandrovna, you are so agitated... Calm yourself, for God's sake...

VERA. (Turning away from him.) Oh, you treat me like a child... You do not even deign to give me a serious answer... You simply want to be rid of me... You are consoling me! (She wants to go, but suddenly stops at the sight of Natalya Petrovna.) Natalya Petrovna...

(Belyaev quickly looks round.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Taking a few steps forward.) Yes, it is I. (She speaks with some effort.) I have come for you, Verochka.

VERA. (Slowly and coldly.) Why did you take it into your head to come just here? You were looking for me, then?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, I was looking for you. You are imprudent, Verochka... I have told you more than once already... And you, Alexei Nikolayich, you have forgotten your promise... You have deceived me.

VERA. Oh, enough at last, Natalya Petrovna, stop!

(Natalya Petrovna looks at her in astonishment.)

Stop speaking to me as to a child... (Lowering her voice.) I am a woman from today... I am a woman just like you.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (In confusion.) Vera...

VERA. (Almost in a whisper.) He did not deceive you... It was not he who sought this meeting with me. He does not love me, you know it, you have nothing to be jealous of.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With growing astonishment.) Vera!

VERA. Believe me... do not be crafty any more. These tricks serve no purpose now... I see through them now. Believe me. I am not, Natalya Petrovna, a ward whom you watch over (ironically) like an elder sister... (She moves closer to her.) For you I am a rival.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Vera, you forget yourself...

VERA. Perhaps... but who brought me to this? I do not myself understand where I find the boldness to speak to you so... Perhaps I speak so because I hope for nothing more, because it pleased you to trample me... and you have succeeded... completely. But listen: I do not intend to be crafty with you, as you were with me... know this: I have told him (pointing to Belyaev) everything.

NATALYA PETROVNA. What could you tell him?

VERA. What? (Ironically.) Everything that I managed to notice. You hoped to draw everything out of me without giving yourself away. You were mistaken, Natalya Petrovna. You relied too much on your strength...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Vera, Vera, come to your senses...

VERA. (In a whisper, moving still closer to her.) Tell me that I am mistaken... Tell me that you do not love him... He told me that he does not love me!

(Natalya Petrovna is silent in confusion. Vera remains motionless for some time and suddenly puts her hand to her forehead.)

VERA. Natalya Petrovna, forgive me... I... I do not know myself... what is the matter with me, forgive me, be indulgent... (She bursts into tears and quickly goes out by the corridor door.)

(Silence.)

BELYAEV. (Going up to Natalya Petrovna.) I can assure you, Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Staring motionless at the floor, stretching out her hand in his direction.) Stop, Alexei Nikolayich. Truly... Vera is right... It is time... time for me to stop being crafty. I am to blame before her, before you; you have the right to despise me.

(Belyaev makes an involuntary movement.)

I have humbled myself in my own eyes. One means remains to win back your respect: frankness, complete frankness, whatever the consequences. Besides, I see you for the last time, I speak with you for the last time. I love you. (She still does not look at him.)

BELYAEV. You, Natalya Petrovna!...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes, I. I love you. Vera was not mistaken and did not deceive you. I fell in love with you from the first day of your coming, but I myself learned of it only since yesterday. I do not intend to justify my behaviour... It was unworthy of me... but at least you can now understand, can forgive me. Yes, I was jealous of Vera; yes, I mentally gave her to Bolshintsov in order to remove her from myself and from you; yes, I took advantage of my years, of my position to find out her secret, and, of course I did not expect this, I gave myself away. I love you, Belyaev; but know that pride alone forces this confession from me... the comedy I have played until now has revolted me at last. You cannot stay here... However, after what I have just said to you, you will probably feel very awkward in my presence and will yourself wish to leave here as soon as possible. I am sure of it. This certainty has given me courage. I confess I did not want you to carry away a bad memory of me. Now you know everything... Perhaps I have hindered you... perhaps, if all this had not happened, you would have come to love Verochka... I have only one excuse, Alexei Nikolayich... All this was not in my power. (She falls silent. She says all this in a fairly even and calm voice, not looking at Belyaev. He is silent. She continues with some agitation, still not looking at him.) You do not answer me?... However, I understand that. You have nothing to say to me... The position of a man who does not love and to whom love is declared is too painful. I thank you for your silence. Believe me, when I told you... that I love you, I was not being crafty as before; I counted on nothing; on the contrary: I wanted at last to throw off the mask to which, I can assure you, I am not accustomed... And, after all, why simper and scheme any longer, when everything is known; why pretend any more, when there is not even anyone to deceive? All is over now between us. I do not detain you any more. You may go from here without saying a word to me, without even taking leave of me. I shall not only not consider this rudeness, on the contrary I shall be grateful to you. There are cases in which delicacy is out of place... worse than roughness. It seems we were not destined to know each other. Farewell. Yes, we were not destined to know each other... but at least I hope that now I have ceased to be in your eyes that oppressive, secretive and crafty creature... Farewell, for ever.

(Belyaev, in agitation, wants to say something and cannot.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. You are not going?

BELYAEV. (Bowing, wanting to go and, after some struggle with himself, coming back.) No, I cannot go...

(Natalya Petrovna looks at him for the first time.)

I cannot go like this!... Listen, Natalya Petrovna, you have just told me... you do not want me to carry away an unfavourable memory of you, but neither do I want you to remember me as a man who... My God! I do not know how to express it... Natalya Petrovna, forgive me... I do not know how to talk to ladies... Until now I have known... quite different women. You say that we were not destined to know each other, but for heaven's sake, could I, a simple, almost uneducated boy, could I even think of drawing near to you? Remember who you are and who I am! Remember, could I dare to think... With your upbringing... But what am I saying about upbringing... Look at me... this old coat, and your perfumed dresses... For heaven's sake! Well, yes! I was afraid of you, I am afraid of you even now... Without any exaggeration, I looked at you as at a higher being, and meanwhile... you, you tell me that you love me... you, Natalya Petrovna! Me!... I feel my heart beating as it has never beaten in my life; it beats not from astonishment alone, my vanity is not merely flattered... not at all!... this is no time for vanity... But I... I cannot go like this, do as you will!

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause, as if to herself.) What have I done!

BELYAEV. Natalya Petrovna, for God's sake, believe me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (In an altered voice.) Alexei Nikolayich, if I did not know you for a noble man, for a man to whom falsehood is inaccessible, I might think God knows what. I might perhaps repent of my frankness. But I believe you. I do not want to hide my feelings from you: I am grateful to you for what you have just said. Now I know why we did not come together... So there was nothing in me itself that repelled you... Only my position... (She stops.) All for the best, of course... but it will now be easier for me to part from you... Farewell. (She wants to go.)

BELYAEV. (After a pause.) Natalya Petrovna, I know that I cannot stay here... but I cannot tell you all that is going on within me. You love me... I am even afraid to utter these words... all this is so new to me... it seems to me I see you, hear you for the first time, but one thing I feel: I must go away... I feel that I cannot answer for anything...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (In a weak voice.) Yes, Belyaev, you must go away... Now, after this explanation, you can go away... And is it really so, in spite of all I have done... Oh, believe me, if I could have suspected even remotely all that you have now told me, this confession, Belyaev, would have died within me... I only wanted to put an end to all misunderstandings, I wanted to repent, to punish myself, I wanted to break the last thread at once. If I could have imagined... (She covers her face.)

BELYAEV. I believe you, Natalya Petrovna, I believe you. Why, I myself a quarter of an hour ago... could I have imagined... Only today, at our last meeting before dinner, I felt for the first time something extraordinary, never felt before, as if someone's hand had gripped my heart, and it grew so hot in my breast... Before, I seemed somehow to shun you, as though I did not even like you; but when you told me today that it had seemed to Vera Alexandrovna... (He stops.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With an involuntary smile of happiness on her lips.) Enough, enough, Belyaev; we must not think of that. We must not forget that we are speaking to each other for the last time... that you leave tomorrow...

BELYAEV. Oh yes! I shall leave tomorrow! Now I still can leave... It will all pass... You see, I do not want to exaggerate... I shall go... and then, what God gives! I shall carry away one memory with me, I shall remember for ever that you loved me... But how was it that I did not know you until now? There you are looking at me now... Did I ever try to avoid your glance?... Was I ever timid in your presence?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Smiling.) You have just told me that you are afraid of me.

BELYAEV. I? (After a pause.) Indeed... I am astonished at myself... I, I speak to you so boldly? I do not recognize myself.

NATALYA PETROVNA. And you are not deceived?...

BELYAEV. In what?

NATALYA PETROVNA. In that you... (Shuddering.) Oh God, what am I doing... Listen, Belyaev... Come to my help... No woman has ever been in such a position. I have no strength left, truly... Perhaps it is so for the best, all is ended at once, but at least we have come to know each other... Give me your hand, and farewell for ever.

BELYAEV. (Taking her by the hand.) Natalya Petrovna... I do not know what to say to you at parting... my heart is so full... God give you... (He stops and presses her hand to his lips.) Farewell. (He wants to go out by the garden door.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Looking after him.) Belyaev...

BELYAEV. (Turning round.) Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After a pause, in a weak voice.) Stay...

BELYAEV. What?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Stay, and let God judge us! (She hides her head in her hands.)

BELYAEV. (Quickly going up to her and stretching out his hands to her.) Natalya Petrovna...

(At this moment the garden door opens and Rakitin appears on the threshold. He looks at the two for some time and suddenly comes up to them.)

RAKITIN. (Loudly.) They are looking for you everywhere, Natalya Petrovna...

(Natalya Petrovna and Belyaev look round.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Taking her hands from her face and as if coming to herself.) Ah, it is you... Who is looking for me?

(Belyaev, embarrassed, bows to Natalya Petrovna and wants to go.)

You are going, Alexei Nikolayich... do not forget, you know...

(He bows to her a second time and goes out into the garden.)

RAKITIN. Arkady is looking for you... I confess I did not expect to find you here... but, passing by...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Smiling.) You heard our voices... I met Alexei Nikolayich here... and had a little explanation with him. Today is evidently a day of explanations, but now we can go home... (She wants to go to the corridor door.)

RAKITIN. (With some agitation.) May I know... what decision...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Pretending surprise.) What decision?... I do not understand you.

RAKITIN. (After a long pause, sadly.) In that case I understand everything.

NATALYA PETROVNA. There it is... Again mysterious hints! Well, yes, I have explained myself with him, and now everything is in order again... It was nonsense, exaggeration... All we talked about, all that, is childishness. It should be forgotten now.

RAKITIN. I am not questioning you, Natalya Petrovna.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With forced ease.) What was it I wanted to say to you... I do not remember. No matter. Let us go. All this is over... it has all passed.

RAKITIN. (Looking at her intently.) Yes, all is over. How vexed with yourself you must be now... for your frankness today... (He turns away.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Rakitin...

(He glances at her again; she evidently does not know what to say.)

You have not yet spoken with Arkady?

RAKITIN. Not at all... I have not had time to prepare... You understand, something must be invented...

NATALYA PETROVNA. How intolerable! What do they want of me? They watch me at every step. Rakitin, truly, I am ashamed before you...

RAKITIN. Oh, Natalya Petrovna, please do not trouble yourself... Why? All this is in the order of things. But how plain it is that Mr. Belyaev is still a novice! And why did he get so confused and run away... However, in time... (Quickly, in a low voice.) you will both learn to pretend... (Aloud.) Let us go.

(Natalya Petrovna wants to go up to him and stops. At this moment Islayev's voice is heard behind the garden door.)

ISLAYEV. (Offstage.) He went in here, you say?

(Immediately afterwards Islayev and Shpigelsky come in.)

ISLAYEV. Yes... here he is. Well, well, well! And Natalya Petrovna is here too! (Going up to her.) What is this? A continuation of today's explanation? The subject must be important.

RAKITIN. I met Natalya Petrovna here...

ISLAYEV. Met her? (Looking round.) What a thoroughfare this is, to think of it!

NATALYA PETROVNA. But you came in here yourself...

ISLAYEV. I came in here because... (He stops.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. You were looking for me?

ISLAYEV. (After a pause.) Yes, I was looking for you. Will you not come back to the house? Tea is ready already. It will be dusk soon.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Taking his hand.) Let us go.

ISLAYEV. (Looking round.) Two good rooms for the gardeners could be made out of this hall, or another servants' hall; what do you think, Shpigelsky?

SHPIGELSKY. Certainly.

ISLAYEV. Let us go through the garden, Natasha. (He goes to the garden door. During this whole scene he has not once looked at Rakitin. On the threshold he half turns.) Gentlemen, what about you? Come and have tea. (He goes out with Natalya Petrovna.)

SHPIGELSKY. (To Rakitin.) Well, Mikhailo Alexandrych, let us go... Give me your arm... It seems we are destined to belong to the rear guard...

RAKITIN. (Heatedly.) Ah, doctor, allow me to tell you, you bore me very much...

SHPIGELSKY. (With feigned good nature.) And how I bore myself, Mikhailo Alexandrych, if only you knew!

(Rakitin smiles involuntarily.)

RAKITIN. Come, come...

(They both go out by the garden door.)

## V · The Parting

The same set as in the first and third acts. Morning. Islayev is sitting at a table looking over papers. He suddenly rises.

ISLAYEV. No! I decidedly cannot work today. It is as if a nail were stuck in my head. (He paces.) I confess I did not expect this; I did not expect that I should be anxious... as I am now. How is one to act here?... that is the question. (He grows thoughtful and suddenly shouts.) Matvei!

MATVEI. (Entering.) What do you order?

ISLAYEV. Call the village elder to me... And tell the diggers on the dam to wait for me... Go.

MATVEI. Yes, sir. (He goes out.)

ISLAYEV. (Going up to the table again and turning over the papers.) Yes... a problem!

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Coming in and approaching Islayev.) Arkasha...

ISLAYEV. Ah! It is you, mamma. How is your health?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Sitting down on the sofa.) I am well, thank God. (She sighs.) I am well. (She sighs still louder.) Thank God. (Seeing that Islayev is not listening to her, she sighs very heavily, with a slight groan.)

ISLAYEV. You are sighing... What is the matter with you?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Sighing again, but more lightly.) Oh, Arkasha, as if you did not know what I am sighing about!

ISLAYEV. What do you mean?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (After a pause.) I am your mother, Arkasha. Of course you are a grown man now, with sense; but all the same I am your mother. A great word: mother!

ISLAYEV. Explain yourself, please.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. You know what I am hinting at, my friend. Your wife, Natasha... of course she is an excellent woman, and her conduct until now has been quite exemplary... but she is still so young, Arkasha! And youth...

ISLAYEV. I understand what you want to say... It seems to you that her relations with Rakitin...

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. God forbid! I did not think at all...

ISLAYEV. You did not let me finish... It seems to you that her relations with Rakitin are not quite clear. These mysterious conversations, these tears, all this seems strange to you.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. And what, Arkasha, did he tell you at last, what were those conversations of theirs about?... He told me nothing.

ISLAYEV. I did not question him, mamma, and he, apparently, is in no great hurry to satisfy my curiosity.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. So what do you intend to do now?

ISLAYEV. I, mamma? Nothing.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. What do you mean, nothing?

ISLAYEV. Just so, nothing.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Rising.) I confess, this astonishes me. Of course you are master in your own house and know better than I what is good and what is bad. But think what the consequences...

ISLAYEV. Mamma, truly, you are troubling yourself needlessly.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. My friend, I am a mother... but however you like. (After a pause.) I confess I came to you with the intention of offering my mediation...

ISLAYEV. (Briskly.) No, on that score I must ask you, mamma, not to trouble yourself... Do me the favour!

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. As you wish, Arkasha, as you wish. I shall not say another word. I have warned you, done my duty, and now I shall keep my mouth shut like a fish.

(A short silence.)

ISLAYEV. Are you not going out anywhere today?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Only I must warn you: you are too trusting, my friend; you judge everyone by yourself! Believe me: real friends are too rare in our day!

ISLAYEV. (Impatiently.) Mamma...

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Well, I am silent, silent! And what am I, an old woman, to say? I have outlived my wits, I dare say! And I was brought up on other principles, and tried to instil them in you... Well, well, go on with your work, I shall not disturb you... I shall go. (She goes to the door and stops.) So?... Well, as you like, as you like! (She goes out.)

ISLAYEV. (Looking after her.) Why is it that people who truly love you take it into their heads to put all their fingers one after another into your wound? And they are convinced that it eases you, that is what is funny! However, I do not blame mother: her intentions are really of the best, and how could she not give advice? But that is not the point... (Sitting down.) How am I to act? (After thinking, he rises.) Eh! The simpler the better! Diplomatic subtleties do not suit me... I should be the first to get tangled in them. (He rings.)

(Matvei comes in.)

Is Mikhail Alexandrovich at home, do you know?

MATVEI. He is at home. I saw him just now in the billiard room.

ISLAYEV. Ah! Well, then ask him to come to me.

MATVEI. Yes, sir. (He goes out.)

ISLAYEV. (Walking up and down.) I am not used to such upsets... I hope they will not be repeated often... Though I am of strong build, I shall not bear this. (He puts his hand to his chest.) Ugh!...

(Rakitin comes in from the ballroom, embarrassed.)

RAKITIN. You called me?

ISLAYEV. Yes... (After a pause.) Michel, you are in my debt, you know.

RAKITIN. I?

ISLAYEV. Of course. Have you forgotten your promise? About... Natasha's tears... and in general... When mother and I came upon you, remember, you told me that there is a secret between you which you wanted to explain?

RAKITIN. Did I say: a secret?

ISLAYEV. You did.

RAKITIN. But what secret can we have? We had a conversation.

ISLAYEV. About what? And why did she cry?

RAKITIN. You know, Arkady... there are such moments in a woman's life... even the happiest...

ISLAYEV. Rakitin, stop, you cannot do it like this. I cannot bear to see you in such a position... Your embarrassment weighs on me more than on yourself. (He takes his hand.) We are old friends, you have known me since childhood: I do not know how to be crafty, and you have always been frank with me. Allow me to put one question to you... I give my word of honour beforehand that I shall not doubt the sincerity of your answer. You love my wife, do you not?

(Rakitin glances at Islayev.)

You understand me, do you love her in such a way... Well, in a word, do you love my wife with a love which it is hard for a husband to confess to?

RAKITIN. (After a pause, in a hollow voice.) Yes, I love your wife... with such a love.

ISLAYEV. (Also after a pause.) Michel, thank you for your frankness. You are a noble man. Well, however, what is to be done now? Sit down, let us consider this matter together.

(Rakitin sits down. Islayev walks about the room.)

ISLAYEV. I know Natasha; I know her worth... But I know my own worth too. I am not worthy of you, Michel... do not interrupt me, please, I am not worthy of you. You are cleverer, better, finally more agreeable than I. I am a simple man. Natasha loves me, I think, but she has eyes... well, in a word, you must please her. And here is what I shall tell you further: I noticed long ago your mutual liking... But I was always sure of you both, and so long as nothing came to the surface... Ah! I do not know how to speak! (He stops.) But after yesterday's scene, after your second meeting in the evening, what is one to do? If I alone had found you, but witnesses were involved: mother, that rogue Shpigelsky... Well, what do you say, Michel, eh?

RAKITIN. You are perfectly right, Arkady.

ISLAYEV. That is not the question... but what is to be done? I must tell you, Michel, that though I am a simple man, I understand this much: it is not right to devour another's life, and there are cases when it is a sin to insist on one's rights. I did not read that in books, brother... it is conscience that speaks. To give her freedom... well, what of it? To give her freedom! Only one must think it over. It is too important.

RAKITIN. (Rising.) I have already thought it all over.

ISLAYEV. How?

RAKITIN. I must go away... I am going away.

ISLAYEV. (After a pause.) You think so?... Right away from here?

RAKITIN. Yes.

ISLAYEV. (Beginning to walk up and down again.) That... what a word you have said! But perhaps you are right. It will be hard for us without you... God knows, perhaps it will not even achieve its end... But you see better, you know best. I suppose you have thought of it rightly. You are dangerous to me, brother... (With a sad smile.) Yes... you are dangerous to me. See what I said just now... about freedom! And yet perhaps I should not survive it! To be without Natasha... (He waves his hand.) And here is another thing, brother: for some time, especially in these last days, I see a great change in her. Some deep, constant agitation has appeared in her, which frightens me. Am I not right, am I not mistaken?

RAKITIN. (Bitterly.) Oh no, you are not mistaken!

ISLAYEV. Well, you see! So you are going?

RAKITIN. Yes.

ISLAYEV. Hm. And how it all broke upon us suddenly! And you had to be so confused when mother and I found you...

MATVEI. (Coming in.) The village elder has come.

ISLAYEV. Let him wait!

(Matvei goes out.)

Michel, you are not going away for long, though? That would be nonsense, brother!

RAKITIN. I do not know, truly... I think... for long.

ISLAYEV. You do not take me for some Othello, do you? Truly, I think that since the world began there has not been such a conversation between two friends! I cannot part from you like this...

RAKITIN. (Shaking his hand.) You will inform me when I may return.

ISLAYEV. But there is no one here to replace you! Not Bolshintsov, surely!

RAKITIN. There are others here...

ISLAYEV. Who? Krinitsyn? That fop? Belyaev, of course, is a good fellow... but he is to you as a star in the sky!

RAKITIN. (Caustically.) You think so? You do not know him, Arkady... Pay attention to him... I advise you... Do you hear? He is a very... very remarkable man!

ISLAYEV. Ha! That is why you and Natasha wanted to take his education in hand! (Glancing at the door.) Ah! and here he comes, it seems... (Hurriedly.) So, my dear fellow, it is settled, you go away... for a short time... in these days... There is no need to hurry, Natasha must be prepared... I shall calm mother... And God give you happiness! You have taken a stone from my heart... Embrace me, my dear! (He embraces him hastily and turns to Belyaev, who is coming in.) Ah... it is you! Well... how are you?

BELYAEV. Thank God, Arkady Sergeich.

ISLAYEV. And where is Kolya?

BELYAEV. He is with Herr Schaaf.

ISLAYEV. Ah... excellent! (He takes his hat.) Well, gentlemen, goodbye. I have not been anywhere yet today, neither to the dam nor to the building... And I have not looked over the papers. (He tucks them under his arm.) Goodbye! Matvei! Matvei! Come with me! (He goes out.)

(Rakitin remains thoughtful at the front of the stage.)

BELYAEV. (Going up to Rakitin.) How do you feel today, Mikhailo Alexandrych?

RAKITIN. Thank you. As usual. And you?

BELYAEV. I am well.

RAKITIN. One can see it!

BELYAEV. What?

RAKITIN. Just so... from your face... Oh! you have put on a new coat today... And what do I see! A flower in your buttonhole.

(Belyaev blushes and tears it out.)

BELYAEV. But why... why, for heaven's sake...

RAKITIN. It is very charming... (After a pause.) By the way, Alexei Nikolayich, if you need anything... I am going to town tomorrow.

BELYAEV. Tomorrow?

RAKITIN. Yes... and from there perhaps to Moscow.

BELYAEV. (In surprise.) To Moscow? But only yesterday, I think, you told me you meant to stay here about a month...

RAKITIN. Yes... but business... a circumstance has arisen...

BELYAEV. And are you going for long?

RAKITIN. I do not know... perhaps for long.

BELYAEV. Allow me to ask, does Natalya Petrovna know your intention?

RAKITIN. No. Why do you ask me about her in particular?

BELYAEV. I? (Somewhat embarrassed.) Just so.

RAKITIN. (After a pause and looking round.) Alexei Nikolayich, it seems there is no one in the room besides us, is it not strange that we play a comedy for each other, eh? What do you think?

BELYAEV. I do not understand you, Mikhailo Alexandrych.

RAKITIN. Really? You truly do not understand why I am going away?

BELYAEV. No.

RAKITIN. That is strange... However, I am ready to believe you. Perhaps you really do not know the reason... Would you like me to tell you why I am going away?

BELYAEV. Do me the favour.

RAKITIN. Well, you see, Alexei Nikolayich, though I count on your discretion, you have just found me with Arkady Sergeich... We had a rather important conversation. It was precisely as a result of that conversation that I decided to go away. And do you know why? I tell you all this because I consider you a noble man... He imagined that I... well, yes, that I love Natalya Petrovna. How does that seem to you, eh? Is it not a strange idea? But I am grateful to him because he did not scheme, did not watch us, but turned to me simply and directly. Now tell me, what would you do in my place? Of course his suspicions have no foundation, but they trouble him... For the peace of his friends a decent man must be able to sacrifice sometimes... his own pleasure. That is why I am going away. I am sure you will approve my decision, will you not? You... you would do just the same in my place, would you not? You too would go away?

BELYAEV. (After a pause.) Perhaps.

RAKITIN. I am very glad to hear it... Of course I do not deny that there is a ridiculous side to my intention to withdraw, as if I myself considered myself dangerous; but you see, Alexei Nikolayich, a woman's honour is such an important thing... And besides, I am of course not speaking of Natalya Petrovna, but I have known women pure and innocent of heart, true children for all their intelligence, who precisely because of this purity and innocence were more than others capable of surrendering to a sudden infatuation... And therefore, who knows? Extra caution in such cases does no harm, the more so as... By the way, Alexei Nikolayich, perhaps you still imagine that love is the highest good on earth?

BELYAEV. (Coldly.) I have not yet experienced it, but I think that to be loved by the woman one loves is a great happiness.

RAKITIN. God grant you long to keep such pleasant convictions! In my opinion, Alexei Nikolayich, every love, happy as well as unhappy, is a real calamity when one gives oneself to it wholly... Wait! perhaps you will yet learn how these tender little hands know how to torture, with what caressing solicitude they tear the heart to pieces... Wait! you will learn how much burning hatred lies hidden under the most ardent love! You will remember me when, as a sick man thirsts for health, you will thirst for peace, the most senseless, the most vulgar peace, when you will envy every carefree and free man... Wait! You will learn what it means to belong to a skirt, to be enslaved, infected, and how shameful and wearisome this slavery is!... You will learn at last what trifles are bought at so high a price... But why am I saying all this to you; you will not believe me now. The point is that your approval is very pleasant to me... yes, yes... in such cases one must be careful.

BELYAEV. (Who has not taken his eyes off Rakitin all the time.) Thank you for the lesson, Mikhailo Alexandrych, though I did not need it.

RAKITIN. (Taking his hand.) Please forgive me, I had no intention... it is not for me to give lessons to anybody... I only got talking...

BELYAEV. (With slight irony.) Without any occasion?

RAKITIN. (Somewhat confused.) Precisely without any particular occasion. I only wanted... Until now, Alexei Nikolayich, you have had no opportunity to study women. Women are a very wayward people.

BELYAEV. But whom are you talking about?

RAKITIN. Just so... no one in particular.

BELYAEV. About all of them in general, is that so?

RAKITIN. (Smiling with an effort.) Yes, perhaps. I truly do not know why I fell into this tutorial tone, but allow me at parting to give you one good piece of advice. (Stopping and waving his hand.) Oh! but what sort of adviser am I! Excuse my chatter, please...

BELYAEV. On the contrary, on the contrary...

RAKITIN. So, you need nothing from town?

BELYAEV. Nothing, thank you. But I am sorry that you are leaving.

RAKITIN. I thank you humbly... Believe me, I am too...

(Natalya Petrovna and Vera come out of the study door; Vera is very sad and pale.)

I was very glad to make your acquaintance... (He shakes his hand again.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Looking at the two of them for some time and coming up to them.) Good morning, gentlemen...

RAKITIN. (Turning quickly.) Good morning, Natalya Petrovna... Good morning, Vera Alexandrovna...

(Belyaev bows silently to Natalya Petrovna and Vera. He is embarrassed.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (To Rakitin.) What are you up to?

RAKITIN. Nothing...

NATALYA PETROVNA. We have already been walking in the garden, Vera and I... It is so lovely in the open air today... The limes smell so sweet. We walked under the limes the whole time... It is pleasant to hear in the shade the humming of the bees overhead... (Timidly, to Belyaev.) We hoped to meet you there.

(Belyaev is silent.)

RAKITIN. (To Natalya Petrovna.) Ah! so you too pay attention today to the beauties of nature... (After a pause.) Alexei Nikolayich could not go into the garden... He put on a new coat today...

BELYAEV. (Flushing slightly.) Of course, since I have only one, and in the garden one might tear it... Is that what you wanted to say?

RAKITIN. (Blushing.) Oh no... I meant nothing of the kind...

(Vera goes silently to the sofa on the right, sits down and takes up her work. Natalya Petrovna smiles at Belyaev with an effort. A short, rather painful silence. Rakitin continues with caustic carelessness.)

Ah yes, I forgot to tell you, Natalya Petrovna, I am leaving today...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With some agitation.) You are leaving? Where?

RAKITIN. To town... On business.

NATALYA PETROVNA. I hope not for long?

RAKITIN. As business goes.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Mind you come back soon. (To Belyaev, not looking at him.) Alexei Nikolayich, were these your drawings that Kolya showed me? Did you draw them?

BELYAEV. Yes... I... trifles...

NATALYA PETROVNA. On the contrary, they are very pretty. You have talent.

RAKITIN. I see you discover new merits in Mr. Belyaev every day.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Coldly.) Perhaps... So much the better for him. (To Belyaev.) You probably have other drawings, you will show them to me.

(Belyaev bows.)

RAKITIN. (Who has been standing on pins all the time.) However, I remember that it is time for me to pack... Goodbye. (He goes to the door of the ballroom.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After him.) But you will still say goodbye to us...

RAKITIN. Of course.

BELYAEV. (After some hesitation.) Mikhailo Alexandrych, wait, I shall go with you. I need to say two words to you...

RAKITIN. Ah!

(They both go into the ballroom. Natalya Petrovna remains in the middle of the stage; after a little while she sits down on the left.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (After some silence.) Vera!

VERA. (Not raising her head.) What do you wish?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Vera, for God's sake, do not be so with me... for God's sake, Vera... Verochka...

(Vera says nothing. Natalya Petrovna rises, crosses the whole stage and quietly kneels before her. Vera tries to raise her, turns away and covers her face. Natalya Petrovna speaks on her knees.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. Forgive me; do not cry, Vera. I am guilty before you, I am guilty. Can you not forgive me?

VERA. (Through tears.) Get up, get up...

NATALYA PETROVNA. I shall not get up, Vera, until you forgive me. It is hard for you... but remember, is it easier for me... remember, Vera... You know everything... Between us there is only this difference, that you are guilty in nothing before me, and I...

VERA. (Bitterly.) Only this difference! No, Natalya Petrovna, there is another difference between us... Today you are so gentle, so kind, so tender...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Interrupting her.) Because I feel my guilt...

VERA. Really? Only for that reason...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Rising and sitting down beside her.) But what other reason can there be?

VERA. Natalya Petrovna, do not torment me any more, do not question me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Sighing.) Vera, I see you cannot forgive me.

VERA. You are so kind and so gentle today because you feel yourself loved.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (In confusion.) Vera!

VERA. (Turning to her.) Well, is it not true?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Sadly.) Believe me, we two are equally unhappy.

VERA. He loves you!

NATALYA PETROVNA. Vera, why should we torment each other? It is time for us both to come to our senses. Remember what position I am in, what position we are both in. Remember that our secret, through my fault, of course, is already known to two people here... (She stops.) Vera, instead of tormenting each other with suspicions and reproaches, would it not be better for us two to think how to get out of this painful position... how to save ourselves! Or do you think that I can bear these agitations, these anxieties? Or have you forgotten who I am? But you are not listening to me.

VERA. (Thoughtfully staring at the floor.) He loves you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. Vera, he will go away.

VERA. (Turning.) Ah, leave me alone...

(Natalya Petrovna looks at her hesitantly. At this moment Islayev's voice is heard in the study.)

ISLAYEV. (Offstage.) Natasha, oh Natasha, where are you?

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Rising quickly and going to the study door.) I am here... What do you want?

ISLAYEV. (Offstage.) Come here, I have something to tell you...

NATALYA PETROVNA. At once.

(She returns to Vera and holds out her hand to her; Vera does not stir. Natalya Petrovna sighs and goes into the study.)

VERA. (Alone, after a silence.) He loves her!... And I must remain in her house... Oh! this is too much... (She covers her face with her hands and remains motionless.)

(Shpigelsky's head appears at the door leading to the ballroom. He looks round cautiously and tiptoes up to Vera, who does not notice him.)

SHPIGELSKY. (After standing before her for a while, with arms folded and a caustic smile on his face.) Vera Alexandrovna!... Oh, Vera Alexandrovna...

VERA. (Raising her head.) Who is it? You, doctor...

SHPIGELSKY. What is the matter, my dear young lady, are you unwell?

VERA. No, nothing.

SHPIGELSKY. Let me feel your pulse. (He feels her pulse.) Hm, why so fast? Ah, my young lady, my young lady... You do not listen to me... And yet I wish you so well.

VERA. (Looking at him resolutely.) Ignaty Ilyich...

SHPIGELSKY. (Quickly.) I am listening, Vera Alexandrovna... What a look, for heaven's sake... I am listening.

VERA. This gentleman... Bolshintsov, your acquaintance, is he really a good man?

SHPIGELSKY. My friend Bolshintsov? An excellent, most honest man... a model and example of virtue.

VERA. Is he not unkind?

SHPIGELSKY. The kindest, I assure you. He is not a man, he is dough, I assure you. You have only to take it and mould it. Such a good soul you would not find by day with a lantern. A dove, not a man.

VERA. Do you vouch for him?

SHPIGELSKY. (Laying one hand on his heart and raising the other.) As for myself!

VERA. In that case you may tell him... that I am ready to marry him.

SHPIGELSKY. (With joyful astonishment.) Really?

VERA. Only as soon as possible, do you hear? as soon as possible...

SHPIGELSKY. Tomorrow, if you like... Of course! Bravo, Vera Alexandrovna! A fine young lady! I shall gallop to him at once. How I shall delight him... What an unexpected turn of events! Why, he adores you, Vera Alexandrovna...

VERA. (Impatiently.) I am not asking you about that, Ignaty Ilyich.

SHPIGELSKY. As you know, Vera Alexandrovna; as you know. Only you will be happy with him, you will thank me, you will see...

(Vera makes another impatient movement.)

Well, I am silent, I am silent... So I may tell him...

VERA. You may, you may.

SHPIGELSKY. Very good. Then I set off at once. Goodbye. (Listening.) And by the way, someone is coming here. (He goes to the study and on the threshold makes an astonished grimace to himself.) Goodbye. (He goes out.)

VERA. (Looking after him.) Anything in the world rather than stay here... (She rises.) Yes; I have decided. I shall not stay in this house... at any price. I cannot bear her gentle look, her smile, I cannot see how she rests wholly, luxuriates wholly in her happiness. For she is happy, however she may pretend to be sad and sorrowful... Her caresses are unbearable to me...

(Belyaev appears at the door of the ballroom. He looks about and goes up to Vera.)

BELYAEV. (In a low voice.) Vera Alexandrovna, are you alone?

VERA. (Looking round, starting, and after a short pause, saying.) Yes.

BELYAEV. I am glad you are alone... Otherwise I should not have come here. Vera Alexandrovna, I have come to say goodbye to you.

VERA. To say goodbye?

BELYAEV. Yes, I am leaving.

VERA. You are leaving? You too are leaving?

BELYAEV. Yes... I too. (With strong inner agitation.) You see, Vera Alexandrovna, I cannot stay here. My presence has already done much harm here. Besides that, I have, I do not know how, disturbed your peace and Natalya Petrovna's peace, I have also broken old, friendly ties. Through me Mr. Rakitin is leaving here, you have quarrelled with your benefactress... It is time to stop all this. After my departure everything, I hope, will grow calm again and fall into order... To turn the heads of rich ladies and young girls is not my business... You will forget me and perhaps in time will wonder how all this could have happened... Even now it amazes me... I do not want to deceive you, Vera Alexandrovna: I am frightened, I feel uneasy staying here... I cannot answer for anything... I am not used to all this, you know. I feel awkward... it seems to me that everyone is looking at me... And finally it will be impossible for me... now... with both of you...

VERA. Oh, do not worry on my account! I shall not stay here long.

BELYAEV. How?

VERA. That is my secret. But I shall not hinder you, believe me.

BELYAEV. Well, you see, how can I help going? Judge for yourself. I have brought a plague into this house, as it were: everyone is running from here... Is it not better for me to vanish alone while there is still time? I have just had a long conversation with Mr. Rakitin... You cannot imagine how much bitterness there was in his words... And he rightly jeered at my new coat... He is right. Yes, I must go. Will you believe me, Vera Alexandrovna, I cannot wait for the moment when I shall be jolting in a cart along the high road... I feel stifled here, I want fresh air. I cannot bear it, so bitter and at the same time so light, like a man setting out on a distant voyage, across the sea: he is sick at heart to part from his friends, he feels uneasy, and yet the sea roars so merrily, the wind blows so fresh in his face that his blood plays in his veins in spite of the heaviness of his heart... Yes, I am going for certain. I shall return to Moscow, to my comrades, I shall work...

VERA. So you love her, Alexei Nikolayich; you love her, and yet you are going away.

BELYAEV. Enough, Vera Alexandrovna, what is the use of this? Do you not see that everything is over. Everything. It flared up and went out like a spark. Let us part as friends. It is time. I have come to my senses. Be well, be happy, we shall meet someday... I shall never forget you, Vera Alexandrovna... I came to like you very much, believe me... (He shakes her hand and adds hurriedly.) Give this note from me to Natalya Petrovna...

VERA. (Glancing at him in confusion.) A note?

BELYAEV. Yes... I cannot say goodbye to her.

VERA. But are you leaving right now?

BELYAEV. Right now... I have told nobody about it... except Mikhail Alexandrych. He approves of me. I shall set out from here on foot to Petrovskoye. At Petrovskoye I shall wait for Mikhail Alexandrych, and we shall go to town together. From town I shall write. My things will be sent after me. You see, everything is already arranged... However, you may read this note. It has only two words.

VERA. (Taking the note from him.) And truly, you are leaving?...

BELYAEV. Yes, yes... Give her this note and say... No, do not say anything to her. What for? (Listening.) Someone is coming. Farewell. (He rushes to the door, stops for a moment on the threshold and runs out.)

(Vera remains with the note in her hand. Natalya Petrovna comes out of the drawing-room.)

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Going up to Vera.) Verochka... (She looks at her and stops.) What is the matter with you?

(Vera silently holds out the note to her.)

A note?... From whom?

VERA. (Dully.) Read it.

NATALYA PETROVNA. You frighten me. (She reads the note to herself and suddenly presses both hands to her face and falls into an armchair.)

(A long silence.)

VERA. (Approaching her.) Natalya Petrovna...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Without taking her hands from her face.) He is going away!... He did not even want to say goodbye to me... Oh! with you he at least said goodbye!

VERA. (Sadly.) He did not love me...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Taking away her hands and rising.) But he has no right to go away like this... I want... He cannot do so... Who allowed him to break off so stupidly... This is contempt, after all... I... how does he know that I should never have resolved... (She sinks into an armchair.) My God, my God!...

VERA. Natalya Petrovna, you yourself told me just now that he must go away... Remember.

NATALYA PETROVNA. It is well for you now... He is going away... Now we two are equal... (Her voice breaks.)

VERA. Natalya Petrovna, you told me just now... these are your own words: instead of tormenting each other, would it not be better for us to think together how to get out of this position, how to save ourselves... We are saved now.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Turning away from her almost with hatred.) Ah...

VERA. I understand you, Natalya Petrovna... Do not worry... I shall not burden you long with my presence. We cannot live together.

NATALYA PETROVNA. (Wanting to hold out her hand to her and letting it fall on her knees.) Why do you say that, Verochka... Do you too want to leave me? Yes, you are right, we are saved now. All is over... everything is in order again...

VERA. (Coldly.) Do not worry, Natalya Petrovna.

(Vera looks at her silently. Islayev comes out of the study.)

ISLAYEV. (After looking at Natalya Petrovna for some time, in a low voice to Vera.) Does she know that he is leaving?

VERA. (Puzzled.) Yes... she knows.

ISLAYEV. (To himself.) But why so soon... (Aloud.) Natasha... (He takes her by the hand.)

(She raises her head.)

It is I, Natasha.

(She tries to smile.)

You are unwell, my dear? I should advise you to lie down, truly...

NATALYA PETROVNA. I am well, Arkady... It is nothing.

ISLAYEV. But you are pale... Truly, listen to me... Rest a little.

NATALYA PETROVNA. Well, perhaps. (She tries to rise and cannot.)

ISLAYEV. (Helping her.) There, you see...

(She leans on his arm.)

Shall I go with you?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Oh! I am not so weak yet! Come, Vera. (She makes for the study.)

(Rakitin comes in from the ballroom. Natalya Petrovna stops.)

RAKITIN. I have come, Natalya Petrovna...

ISLAYEV. (Interrupting him.) Ah, Michel! Come here! (He draws him aside and says in a low voice, with vexation.) Why did you tell her everything at once? I think I asked you! What was the need to hurry... I found her here in such agitation...

RAKITIN. (In astonishment.) I do not understand you.

ISLAYEV. You told Natasha that you are leaving...

RAKITIN. So you suppose that she became agitated because of that?

ISLAYEV. Sh! She is looking at us. (Aloud.) Are you not going to your room, Natasha?

NATALYA PETROVNA. Yes... I am going...

RAKITIN. Goodbye, Natalya Petrovna!

(Natalya takes hold of the door handle and answers nothing.)

ISLAYEV. (Laying his hand on Rakitin's shoulder.) Natasha, do you know, this is one of the best of men...

NATALYA PETROVNA. (With sudden impulse.) Yes, I know, he is an excellent man, you are all excellent men... all, all... and yet...

(She suddenly covers her face with her hands, pushes the door open with her knee and quickly goes out. Vera goes out after her. Islayev sits down silently at the table and leans on his elbows.)

RAKITIN. (Looking at him for some time and with a bitter smile shrugging his shoulders.) What a position! Splendid, I must say. Truly, even refreshing. And what a parting, after four years of love? Good, very good, it serves the chatterbox right. And, thank God, all is for the best. It was time to end this morbid, this consumptive relationship. (Aloud to Islayev.) Well, Arkady, goodbye.

ISLAYEV. (Raising his head. He has tears in his eyes.) Goodbye, brother. And it is... not quite easy. I did not expect it, brother. Like a storm on a clear day. Well, it will grind itself out... there will be flour. But all the same, thank you, thank you! You are a friend, truly!

RAKITIN. (Aside, through his teeth.) This is too much. (Abruptly.) Goodbye.

(Rakitin wants to go into the ballroom. Shpigelsky runs in to meet him.)

SHPIGELSKY. What is it? I was told Natalya Petrovna feels ill...

ISLAYEV. (Rising.) Who told you?

SHPIGELSKY. A girl... a maid...

ISLAYEV. No, it is nothing, doctor. I think it is better not to disturb Natasha now...

SHPIGELSKY. Well, splendid! (To Rakitin.) You are going to town, they say?

RAKITIN. Yes; on business.

SHPIGELSKY. Ah! On business!...

(At this moment Anna Semyonovna, Lizaveta Bogdanovna, Kolya and Schaaf burst in all together from the ballroom.)

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. What is it? What is it? What is the matter with Natasha?

KOLYA. What is the matter with mamma? What is the matter with her?

ISLAYEV. Nothing is the matter with her... I have just seen her... What is the matter with you?

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. But for heaven's sake, Arkasha, we were told that Natasha was unwell...

ISLAYEV. You were wrong to believe it.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Why are you so hot, Arkasha? Our concern is understandable.

ISLAYEV. Of course... of course...

RAKITIN. However, it is time for me to go.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. You are leaving?

RAKITIN. Yes... I am leaving.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (To herself.) Ah! Well, now I understand.

KOLYA. (To Islayev.) Papa...

ISLAYEV. What do you want?

KOLYA. Why has Alexei Nikolayich gone away?

ISLAYEV. Gone where?

KOLYA. I do not know... He kissed me, put on his cap and went away... And now it is the hour of the Russian lesson.

ISLAYEV. He will probably come back at once... However, we can send for him.

RAKITIN. (In a low voice to Islayev.) Do not send for him, Arkady. He will not come back.

(Anna Semyonovna tries to overhear. Shpigelsky whispers with Lizaveta Bogdanovna.)

ISLAYEV. What does this mean?

RAKITIN. He is leaving too.

ISLAYEV. Leaving... where?

RAKITIN. To Moscow.

ISLAYEV. To Moscow! What, is everyone going mad today?

RAKITIN. (Lowering his voice still more.) Between ourselves... Verochka has fallen in love with him... Well, he, as an honourable man, has resolved to withdraw.

(Islayev, spreading his arms, sinks into an armchair.)

You understand now why...

ISLAYEV. (Jumping up.) I? I understand nothing. My head is going round. What is there to understand? Everyone is scampering off every which way, like partridges, and all because honest people... And all this at once, on one and the same day...

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Coming up from the side.) But what is it? Mr. Belyaev, you say...

ISLAYEV. (Shouting nervously.) Nothing, mother, nothing! Herr Schaaf, be so good as to take Kolya now in place of Mr. Belyaev. Be so good as to take him away.

SCHAAF. As you wish, sir... (He takes Kolya by the hand.)

KOLYA. But, papa...

ISLAYEV. (Shouting.) Go, go!

(Schaaf leads Kolya away.)

And you, Rakitin, I shall see you off... I shall order a horse saddled, I shall wait for you at the dam... And you, mother, for God's sake do not disturb Natasha for now, and you too, doctor... Matvei! Matvei! (He goes out hurriedly.)

(Anna Semyonovna sits down with dignity and sadness. Lizaveta Bogdanovna takes her stand behind her. Anna Semyonovna raises her eyes to heaven, as though wishing to disown everything that is going on around her.)

SHPIGELSKY. (Stealthily and slyly, to Rakitin.) And what, Mikhailo Alexandrych, would you like me to drive you on my new little troika as far as the high road?

RAKITIN. Ah!... Have you already received the horses?

SHPIGELSKY. (Modestly.) I have spoken with Vera Alexandrovna... So do you wish it?

RAKITIN. Very well! (Bowing to Anna Semyonovna.) Anna Semyonovna, I have the honour...

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Still as majestically, not rising from her seat.) Farewell, Mikhailo Alexandrych... I wish you a happy journey...

RAKITIN. I thank you humbly. Lizaveta Bogdanovna...

(He bows to her. She curtsies in reply. He goes into the ballroom.)

SHPIGELSKY. (Going up to kiss Anna Semyonovna's hand.) Farewell, madam...

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Less majestically, but still severely.) Ah! And you are leaving too, doctor?

SHPIGELSKY. Yes, ma'am... The patients, you know. Besides, you see, my presence is not required here. (Bowing, he slyly squints at Lizaveta Bogdanovna, who answers him with a smile.) Goodbye... (He runs out after Rakitin.)

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. (Letting him go out and, folding her arms, slowly turning to Lizaveta Bogdanovna.) What do you think of all this, my dear, eh?

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Sighing.) I do not know what to say to you, Anna Semyonovna.

ANNA SEMYONOVNA. Have you heard that Belyaev is leaving too...

LIZAVETA BOGDANOVNA. (Sighing again.) Ah, Anna Semyonovna, perhaps I too shall not stay here long... I am leaving too.

(Anna Semyonovna looks at her with inexpressible amazement; Lizaveta Bogdanovna stands before her, not raising her eyes.)
